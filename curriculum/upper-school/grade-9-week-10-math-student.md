# Grade 9 mathematics: a common solution

**Five sessions, 60 minutes each.** Review draft for the existing Grade 9 Week
10 Algebra I sequence, not an approved full-year course.

**Goal:** graph and interpret a pair of linear equations, verify a proposed
intersection in both equations, and distinguish one, no and infinitely many
solutions.

Use paper, a ruler and a pencil or an accessible graphing/table tool.
An adult can read the tasks exactly or record your dictated response. During
independent checks the adult records assistance but does not supply the method.

## Day 1: a point must work twice

1. **Retrieve — 10 minutes.** Explain what each coordinate in `(2, 1)` means.
   Evaluate `3x − y` and `x + y` at that point.
2. **Learn — 10 minutes.** With your adult, read Example 165 in the separate
   [Wallace reference](wallace-4-1-review-extract.md). Close it afterward.
3. **Guided practice — 15 minutes.** For `y = x + 2` and `y = 2x`, test
   `(1, 3)`, `(2, 4)` and `(3, 5)`. Make a two-column record: “works in the
   first equation” and “works in the second equation.”
4. **Explain — 15 minutes.** A learner says, “The point works in one equation,
   so it solves the system.” Give a counterexample from your record.
5. **Exit — 10 minutes.** Independently test whether `(3, 5)` solves both
   `x + y = 8` and `2x − y = 1`. Show both substitutions.

## Day 2: find the shared point graphically

1. **Retrieve — 10 minutes.** Explain yesterday's two-equation check.
2. **Learn — 10 minutes.** Read Example 166 in the Wallace reference. Explain
   how two points locate each straight line; then put the reference away.
3. **Guided graph — 15 minutes.** Graph `y = x + 1` and `y = −x + 5`. Start
   each table with `x = 0, 1, 2, 3`; label both axes and lines.
4. **Practice — 15 minutes.** Estimate the common point. Check it in both
   equations. Explain what an inaccurate scale could do to the estimate.
5. **Exit — 10 minutes.** Independently graph `y = 2x + 1` and `y = −x + 7`
   and verify the common point. A table of coordinates is an alternative if
   drawing is inaccessible; describe where the same pair occurs.

## Day 3: use the intersection to make a decision

1. **Retrieve — 10 minutes.** Why is a drawn crossing not enough to certify
   an exact answer?
2. **Model — 10 minutes.** Two fictional printing options cost `A = 6 + 2x`
   and `B = 18 + x` dollars for `x` whole packets. Discuss what the constant
   and coefficient mean. Compare their values at `x = 0` and `x = 12`.
3. **Guided table and graph — 15 minutes.** Make tables using
   `x = 0, 4, 8, 12, 16`. Graph both models and locate their common value.
4. **Reason — 15 minutes.** Choose the cheaper option for 8 packets and for
   16 packets. Explain why the answer changes, and why a negative packet
   count is not meaningful in this context.
5. **Exit — 10 minutes.** Two different options cost `C = 4 + 3x` and
   `D = 16 + x`. Find and verify the common packet count and cost. State
   which is cheaper below that count.

## Day 4: not every system has one solution

1. **Retrieve — 10 minutes.** What does “common solution” mean?
2. **Investigate — 15 minutes.** Draw or tabulate `y = 2x + 1` and
   `y = 2x − 3`. Compare their slopes and vertical separation.
3. **Investigate — 15 minutes.** Rewrite `2y = 4x + 2`. Compare its points
   with `y = 2x + 1`. Decide whether these are distinct lines.
4. **Classify — 10 minutes.** Give a reason, not just a label, for each:
   - `y = x + 2` and `y = −x + 4`;
   - `y = 3x + 1` and `y = 3x + 7`;
   - `y = −2x + 5` and `2y = −4x + 10`.
5. **Exit — 10 minutes.** Create a different example with no solution and
   explain why changing only the common slope would not fix the separation
   if both slopes were changed to that same value.

## Day 5: independent demonstration and feedback

Use the first 25 minutes independently. Graph paper, enlarged print, exact
read-aloud, and an accessible table tool are allowed. Do not consult the
reference or adult key. The adult records every content hint separately.

1. Test `(2, 5)` against `y = 2x + 1` and `x + y = 7`; show both checks.
2. Graph `y = −x + 6` and `y = x + 2`. State and verify their common point.
3. Classify `y = 4x + 1` and `y = 4x − 2`. Explain graphically.
4. Classify `2y = 6x + 8` and `y = 3x + 4`. Explain algebraically.
5. **New application.** Containers begin with 3 L and 15 L. At the ends of
   successive whole minutes their modelled volumes increase by 5 L and 2 L
   respectively. Write both models; graph or tabulate them; find when and
   at what volume they match. These are supplied mathematical models, not
   instructions to run a water experiment.

**Next 20 minutes:** explain your reasoning to the adult. Distinguish your first
answers from corrected work.

**Final 15 minutes:** correct one error with a reason, or create and solve a
different system if your first work was secure.

## Delayed check: seven days later

Spend 10 minutes within next week's mathematics allocation, not as additional
homework. Without previous examples or hints:

1. Graph or tabulate `y = x + 3` and `y = −x + 9`. Verify the intersection.
2. Explain why `y = 5x + 2` and `y = 5x − 1` have no common solution.

## Ask Bede for guidance

Useful prompts:

- “Help me choose two points for each line. Ask me before showing a step.”
- “I found a point. Ask me how I can check it in both equations.”
- “Ask a question that helps me distinguish parallel and coincident lines.”

Bede should ask for your attempt and offer one targeted question at a time.
Tell the adult if a tool supplies a completed answer during an independent
check; that response can be discussed, but is not evidence of unaided work.
