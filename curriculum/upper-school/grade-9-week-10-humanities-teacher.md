# Grade 9 Week 10 humanities — adult-only review and keys

Keep separate from learner retrieval and independent assessment. This
companion belongs with grade-9-week-10-humanities.md. It does not establish

## Literature

Pedagogical intent: an interpretation should respond to new evidence without
confusing narrator knowledge, character belief and established events.

Three progressive questions: What does this speaker know? What passage
challenges your claim? Which narrower claim accounts for both passages?

Day 1: concealing identity does not logically establish dishonesty in every
action. Accept retaining a claim when evidence supports it; do not reward
changing an opinion for its own sake.
Day 2: Eurycleia recognizes Odysseus through the scar; Penelope is not thereby
automatically informed. Require the learner to locate the recognition scene
and distinguish character knowledge from the reader's earlier knowledge.
Day 3: dreams, interpretations and proposed future actions have different
evidential status. A claim about Penelope's motives may be arguable rather
than settled; reward a defensible interpretation with textual support.
Day 4: check every quotation in the actual selected edition. Do not credit
an adult summary as direct quotation or a character's omen interpretation as
independently established fact.

Day 5 rubric, 0–2 each:
1. Arguable claim with appropriate scope.
2. Two accurately contextualized XIX–XX references and one earlier reference.
3. Explanation connecting references to the argument.
4. Fair counterargument with a reasoned response.
5. Revision memo and coherent writing.

2 = secure and explained; 1 = partial or needs prompting; 0 = absent or
materially wrong. Provisional progression: 8/10 with no fabricated evidence
and a correctly contextualized counterargument. Repair the specific weakness
and reassess with a new paragraph. This threshold is instructional, not a
validated mastery score. Score oral and written evidence separately.
Delayed check: score claim, contrary evidence and justified response; no
required opinion. Adult records the unfamiliar passage used.

## History

Pedagogical intent: historical explanations must distinguish observations,
interpretations and limits of sources.

Three progressive questions: What was actually found or written? Which
explanations could fit it? What new evidence could distinguish them?

Day 1: the Yellow River system is generally north of the Yangtze system;
both flow broadly toward China's eastern coast. The schematic must not imply
exact scale or uniform settlement. Rivers can support transport and water
access while presenting flooding or crossing challenges. Geography alone
does not prove political unity, inevitable farming or a single culture.
Day 2: accept only claims that the learner can locate in the bounded prose.
If evidence is unspecified there, that is an accurate limitation, not failure.
Day 3: do not invent dates or force a detailed dynasty list into an
insufficient excerpt. Mark uncertain/traditional chronology as such. External
details require separate inspected sources before becoming teaching claims.
Day 4: similar vessels could reflect exchange, shared production techniques,
imitation or common administration; similarity alone cannot select one.
A storage building suggests storage capacity, not automatically centralized
taxation or a named ruler. Dating, distribution, production evidence and
independently evaluated records could help distinguish hypotheses. A much
later story needs its own context and corroboration.

Day 5 rubric, 0–2 each: accurate bounded references; readable labelled
schematic; cautious geography explanation; observation/inference separation;
explicit evidence limitation. Use the same 2/1/0 meanings as literature.
Provisional progression is 8/10 with no invented citations and no presentation
of the practice dossier as real archaeology. Reteach and recheck a fresh
dossier. Delayed road dossier: shared engineering, imitation, trade or unified
administration are possibilities; dating, connections and independently
evaluated inscriptions/records could help. No single expected empire name.

## Latin

Pedagogical intent: agreement follows gender, number and case rather than
surface spelling or word order.

Three progressive questions: What is the noun's dictionary gender? What
role and number does the sentence require? Which adjective form meets all
three conditions?

Day 1: dōnum bonum; puella parva; nautae bonī; dōna parva.
Nauta is masculine nominative singular despite its first-declension ending;
bonus therefore uses masculine nominative singular.
Day 2: (1) The good sailor likes the story. (2) The girl likes the good sailor.
(3) The good sailors carry small gifts. Accept natural articles and “loves”
for amat/“like” for amant where appropriate. Moving an adjective does not
license changing its case.
Day 3: puellae bonae dōna parva portant. Choices bonī, parva, bonās.
Bona alone can be feminine nominative singular or neuter nominative/accusative
plural among taught forms; context selects the parse.
Day 4 passage: The good girl likes the story. The good sailor carries a small
gift. The good girls carry small gifts. The good sailors like stories.
Repairs: nauta bonus; dōna parva; puellae bonae. Check new sentences only
against taught vocabulary and forms, not an automated translation model.

Day 5:
1. bonus: masculine, nominative, singular, agreeing with nauta.
2. The girl likes the good sailors. Nautās bonōs: masculine accusative plural,
   direct object. Puella is singular subject; amat is singular verb.
3. Puellae bonae dōna parva portant.
4. dōna parva: neuter accusative plural if an object, or nominative plural
   in a different construction. The isolated phrase does not decide its role.
5. Nautae bonī dōnum parvum portant. Other intelligible orders acceptable.

Two points per item: correct form/translation (1), appropriate grammatical
explanation (1). Ask for explanation on items 3 and 5 in the conference.
Provisional progression 8/10, including correct masculine nauta agreement
and neuter plural agreement. Do not equate a minor handwriting difficulty
with a grammatical misunderstanding. Retain vowel quantities in teaching;
separately record errors in agreement versus macron copying.

Delayed: The good girls like the good sailor. Puellae bonae is feminine
nominative plural subject; nautam bonum masculine accusative singular object;
amant third-person plural. Object plural: nautās bonōs puellae bonae amant.
Remedial: three-feature cards, then a fresh phrase. Extension: explain two
possible isolated parses and invent contexts disambiguating them.
