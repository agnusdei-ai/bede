# Grade 9 Week 10 — literature, history and Latin

Original Bede teaching draft for local review, not approved course completion.
Each subject has five 60-minute sessions. Reading, discussion, practice and
assessment fit inside those sessions; no additional homework is assumed.
Use the existing Grade 9 source register. No textbook text or pictures are
reproduced here. The adult-only companion contains keys and scoring guidance.

## Literature — revising an interpretation

**Objective:** revise an arguable claim when later narrative evidence changes
its scope. Distinguish what a character knows from what the reader knows.
Prerequisite: bring the Week 9 essay and identify its claim and one supporting
scene. If the claim is only a plot summary, first convert it into a claim
someone could contest.

Reading: Samuel Butler's *Odyssey*, Books XIX–XX, in the existing selected
edition. Use scene boundaries rather than page numbers. Independent reading
is capped at 20 minutes per session. The adult may read aloud or summarize
the remainder, recording which passages were actually read. Summaries do not
count as quotations. Preview threats and violence; use non-graphic dialogue
and adult summary where needed without changing the evidence objective.

### Day 1 — a claim can become too broad (60 minutes)

10m retrieve the Week 9 claim; 20m read XIX from the opening through
Penelope's questioning of the disguised visitor, stopping before the foot
washing; 15m model claim scope; 10m evidence log; 5m exit.

Teaching model using an original invented miniature, not Homer: a visitor
refuses to give her name but returns a lost tool. “The visitor conceals her
name” is an observation; “she is untrustworthy in every respect” adds an
unsupported generalization. A narrower interpretation distinguishes identity
concealment from conduct.

Practice: locate one statement the visitor makes in XIX and record speaker,
audience, scene and what the reader can independently check. Ask, “Does the
statement support my claim, complicate it, or leave it unchanged?”
Exit: replace one absolute word in your own claim only if the evidence
requires it; explain the change rather than merely weakening the sentence.

### Day 2 — recognition and unequal knowledge (60 minutes)

10m retrieve; 20m read the foot-washing/recognition scene in XIX; 15m guided
annotation; 10m independent paragraph; 5m exit.

Make three labelled lists: what Eurycleia knows, what Penelope knows at this
point, and what the reader knows. Put a scene reference beside each entry.
Do not assume recognition by one character automatically informs another.
Write 120–160 words explaining how unequal knowledge affects a reader's
judgment. A plausible interpretation still needs a located passage.

Socratic progression: Who receives the evidence? Who is absent or unaware?
Which sentence in your paragraph assumes more knowledge than the scene gives?

### Day 3 — possibilities are not established events (60 minutes)

10m retrieve; 20m finish XIX, including the dream and proposed contest;
15m discuss; 10m revise evidence log; 5m exit.

Distinguish a reported dream, a character's interpretation and an event
established by the narrative. Label each entry accordingly. Identify one
interpretation the text permits and one claim it does not yet establish.
Return to your Week 9 argument: draft a two-sentence counterargument using
the new material. Do not treat a dream's interpretation as independently
verified history or a prediction as something already accomplished.

### Day 4 — testing the revised claim (60 minutes)

5m retrieve; 20m read XX in selected scenes of waiting, household conduct and
responses to warnings; 15m finish with adult summary as needed; 15m plan;
5m exit.

Choose one conduct scene and one response to a warning. Record who speaks
and how others respond; distinguish narrator report from character belief.
Prepare a revision plan with old claim, new claim, two XIX–XX references,
one earlier reference and the strongest complication. Cross out a reference
that merely repeats the claim without supporting it. No invented quotations.

### Day 5 — independent revision (60 minutes)

35m revise a 400–600-word portion of the quarter essay; 10m write a change
memo; 10m adult conference; 5m record next step. Draft planning on Day 4
replaces, rather than adds to, this allocation.

Submit old and new claims, accurate scene references, a supported
counterargument and a memo explaining why the revision improves the argument.
In the conference explain one sentence without reading the essay aloud.
An oral response may demonstrate interpretation, but is not by itself
evidence of independent written composition.

**Delayed check:** seven days later, replace 10 minutes of the next week's
practice with an unfamiliar short passage selected by the adult. State a
claim, identify contrary evidence, then retain, narrow or reject the claim
with a reason. Judge the reasoning, not whether the opinion changed.

## History — geography, chronology and limits of evidence

**Objective:** explain how location and different kinds of evidence shape
accounts of early China without treating geography as destiny or tradition
as a complete, uncontested chronology.

Source: existing H10, UNG *World History: Cultures, States, and Societies to
1500*, §4.5 **Geography of East Asia** and §4.6 **China from Neolithic Village
Settlements to the Shang Kingdom**. Read the first four author-prose
paragraphs directly under each named heading, excluding captions, embedded
quotations, images and nested subsections. Adult verifies headings against
the lawful edition before use. Cap each selection at 20 minutes; read aloud
or summarize within that time. Do not assign an absent map or claim an
unread subsection supports an answer.

Entry: distinguish a physical object, a later written account and a modern
historian's interpretation. If needed sort three original example cards:
an excavated pot; a story written centuries later; an explanation comparing
both. None alone supplies every detail of the past.

### Day 1 — a useful map makes limited claims (60 minutes)

10m entry; 20m §4.5 selection; 15m adult-guided schematic; 10m explanation;
5m exit. Draw a north arrow, coastline and labelled Yellow and Yangtze river
systems using adult-verified geographic guidance. Label the drawing
“schematic, not to scale”; do not copy excluded textbook artwork.

Write two questions: how might rivers support settlement or movement, and
what difficulties might they create? Do not infer that everyone near a river
farmed, shared a language or obeyed one ruler. Exit: identify one fact the
map shows and one political conclusion it cannot establish.

### Day 2 — different evidence answers different questions (60 minutes)

10m retrieve; 20m §4.6 selection; 15m source log; 10m comparison; 5m exit.
For two statements actually present in the assigned prose, record section,
paragraph number, claim and the kind of supporting evidence the author
identifies. Write “not specified in this selection” if necessary.
Distinguish the modern textbook from any earlier source it discusses.
Do not transform a modern explanation into an ancient eyewitness account.

### Day 3 — sequence without invented precision (60 minutes)

10m retrieve; 15m reread; 20m timeline; 10m discuss; 5m exit.
Build a timeline only from dates or relative sequences supplied in the
assigned selection. Mark approximate dates as approximate. If a named
dynasty or detail is outside the selection, leave it as a research question
rather than silently importing a date. Use separate labels for archaeological
evidence and later tradition where the source distinguishes them.
Explain why an orderly timeline can conceal uncertainty.

### Day 4 — compare hypotheses (60 minutes)

10m retrieve; 10m teaching model; 20m analysis; 15m paragraph; 5m exit.
The following is an **invented practice dossier**, not evidence about China:
two river settlements contain similarly shaped vessels; one has a large
storage building; a much later story attributes both to one ruler.

Propose two explanations for the similar vessels. Name evidence that could
help distinguish exchange, shared techniques or common administration.
Explain what the storage building supports and what it does not prove.
Write a 150-word cautious account with explicit observation/inference labels.
Then compare the method, not the invented facts, with one H10 source question.

### Day 5 — independent historical account (60 minutes)

30m produce a 200–300-word explanation plus annotated schematic; 15m
conference; 10m correction; 5m record. Include two accurately located H10
references, one geographic relationship stated cautiously and one explicit
evidence limitation. Explain how a later tradition could be valuable without
being a complete literal record of an earlier period.

**Delayed check:** within next week's allocation, spend 10 minutes on an
unfamiliar invented dossier: a road segment, a similar road elsewhere and a
later claim of one empire. State one supported observation, two possible
explanations and one useful additional source. Similarity alone is not
proof of political unity.

## Latin — agreement is not matching the final letters

**Objective:** select a first/second-declension adjective agreeing with its
noun in gender, number and case, including masculine first-declension nouns.
Prerequisites: first/second-declension nominative and accusative forms;
present third-person singular/plural verbs. Use original teaching below;
D'Ooge is optional adult reference, not an additional reading assignment.

Vocabulary: puella, puellae f. girl; nauta, nautae m. sailor; dōnum, dōnī n.
gift; fābula, fābulae f. story; bonus/bona/bonum good;
parvus/parva/parvum small; amat loves/likes, amant love/like;
portat carries, portant carry; est is, sunt are.
Dictionary gender belongs to the noun; it is not determined by the spelling
of a particular ending. No inference about a learner's identity is involved.

Reference forms for bonus, limited to this week's cases:
- Masculine nominative: singular bonus, plural bonī.
- Masculine accusative: singular bonum, plural bonōs.
- Feminine nominative: singular bona, plural bonae.
- Feminine accusative: singular bonam, plural bonās.
- Neuter nominative/accusative: singular bonum, plural bona.
Parvus follows the same ending pattern. Dōna is neuter plural; nautās is
masculine accusative plural. Nominative nautae is masculine plural here;
in other contexts that spelling can represent other cases.

### Day 1 — three questions before an ending (60 minutes)

10m entry parsing puella fābulam amat; 15m model; 20m guided practice;
10m independent task; 5m exit.
Model: puella is feminine nominative singular, so “good girl” is puella bona.
Nauta is masculine nominative singular, so “good sailor” is nauta bonus,
not nauta bona. Ask gender, number and case before comparing endings.
Practice with the reference: good gift; small girl; good sailors; small gifts.
Independent: explain why nauta bonus has different endings.

### Day 2 — sentence roles constrain agreement (60 minutes)

10m retrieval; 10m model; 20m practice; 15m explanation; 5m exit.
Model puella bona dōnum parvum portat: identify the subject and direct object
before translating. The noun–adjective pairs agree even when their positions
change. Parse and translate: (1) nauta bonus fābulam amat;
(2) puella nautam bonum amat; (3) nautae bonī dōna parva portant.
Then move one adjective without changing its form or meaning.

### Day 3 — number is not optional (60 minutes)

10m retrieval; 15m guided conversion; 20m practice; 10m independent; 5m exit.
Convert puella bona dōnum parvum portat to plural girls carrying plural gifts.
Check the verb as well as both noun–adjective pairs.
Practice: choose bonus/bonī/bonōs after nominative plural nautae;
choose parva/parvum/parvās after accusative plural dōna;
choose bona/bonae/bonās after accusative plural puellās.
Explain why bona can describe a feminine singular subject or neuter plural
gifts in different contexts. Do not give an isolated form a unique parse.

### Day 4 — read, then repair (60 minutes)

10m retrieve; 15m read; 20m diagnosis; 10m new composition; 5m exit.
Read this original passage:
Puella bona fābulam amat. Nauta bonus dōnum parvum portat.
Puellae bonae dōna parva portant. Nautae bonī fābulās amant.
Underline each subject and circle each direct object.

Repair and explain: nauta bona dōnum portat; puella dōna parvum portat;
puellae bona dōnum portant. Compose two new sentences using only taught
vocabulary, then identify their subjects, objects and adjective agreement.

### Day 5 — independent reading and production (60 minutes)

25m independent items; 15m adult conference; 15m correction; 5m record.
1. Supply “good” in nauta ___ dōnum portat and explain all three features.
2. Translate puella nautās bonōs amat; parse nautās bonōs.
3. Make puella bona dōnum parvum portat entirely plural.
4. Correct dōna parvum and explain the rule.
5. Compose a sentence meaning “The good sailors carry a small gift.”

**Delayed check:** replace 10 minutes next week with translation and parsing
of nautam bonum puellae bonae amant. Identify the subject by endings and verb,
not first position. Then change only the object to plural.

## Access, teaching and tutoring boundaries

Read aloud without adding interpretations; use large print, learner-directed
scribe, tactile maps and labelled endings. Preserve the difference between
oral reasoning and independent writing. Remedial support: reduce the number
of passages/forms and model one example, then use a fresh attempt. On-level:
complete the stated sequence. Extension: test a competing interpretation or
ambiguous form; more work is not required after the session ends.

Homeschool/co-op adults lead short models and individual conferences; charter
teachers may use paired rehearsal followed by individual assessment. Record
each learner's response separately. Estimated adult preparation per subject
is 20m plus approximately 15m guided interaction on each of five days and 5m
delayed scoring: 100m. These are unvalidated planning estimates, not staffing
promises. Learners' 300m per subject includes adult interaction.

Bede asks one scaffold at a time and does not supply essays, translations or
completed independent answers. Educator mode: state pedagogical intent, offer
three progressive questions, then remedial/on-level/extension adaptations.
The separate adult key is not learner retrieval content.
