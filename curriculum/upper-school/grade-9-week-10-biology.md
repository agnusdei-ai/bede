# Grade 9 Week 10 biology — keeping track through meiosis

Local review draft; original Bede instruction. Five sessions total 300 minutes.
Use the existing [B10 explanation](../grade-9/resources/biology-reference-notes.md)
and its S5/S16 factual references. No external text or illustration is copied.
This is a paper-model investigation, not observation of living cells or a
certified laboratory practical.

## Objective, entry check and materials

Distinguish homologous chromosomes from sister chromatids; explain one DNA
replication followed by two divisions; track chromosome and chromatid numbers
in a diploid model with two homologous pairs. Explain how the model differs
from a living cell and how meiosis differs from mitosis.

Entry: ask the learner to distinguish a cell, a chromosome and DNA, and to
explain what “a pair” means. If those concepts are insecure, replace the first
practice block with a labelled cell/nucleus/chromosome sketch and a new check.
Do not use personal family histories or genetic-test data.

Materials: paper, pencil and four spaces labelled as cells. Draw tokens rather
than cutting them. Use written identifiers and shape/texture as well as color:
pair 1 has homologs A and a; pair 2 has B and b. Here these are **chromosome
identifiers**, not claims that whole chromosomes are single alleles or that
capital letters confer dominance.

## Student sequence

### Day 1 — related is not identical (60 minutes)

10m entry check; 10m read B10 with adult explanation; 15m worked model;
15m guided matching; 10m independent exit.

Model: the starting diploid cell has two homologous pairs: A/a and B/b.
Homologs carry corresponding gene locations but may have different versions.
Before DNA replication, represent each chromosome with one line. After
replication, represent each with two attached lines joined at one centromere.
The two joined copies are sister chromatids, not a new homologous pair.

Guided: draw A and a before and after replication. Label the homologs,
chromatids and centromeres. Explain why four drawn lines after replication do
not mean four independently counted chromosomes in this two-chromosome pair.

Exit: for the complete A/a/B/b cell before and after replication, give the
number of homologous pairs, chromosomes and chromatids. State your counting
convention: count chromosomes by centromeres.

### Day 2 — separating homologs (30 minutes)

5m retrieve; 10m adult model; 10m practice; 5m exit.

At division I, homologous chromosomes separate. In one possible arrangement,
the first product receives duplicated A and duplicated B; the other receives
duplicated a and duplicated b. Sisters remain together. This is not the only
possible assortment.

Practice: draw the two products. Identify one alternative assortment that
still gives each product one member of each homologous pair. Do not place A
and a together while omitting pair 2.

Exit: explain why each product has one chromosome set even though its
chromosomes are still duplicated.

### Day 3 — paper investigation (90 minutes)

10m setup; 20m replication model; 20m division I; 20m division II;
10m count table; 10m limitations and cleanup.

Start again with A/a/B/b. Draw replication once, then carry out division I as
above. **Do not replicate again** between divisions. At division II, separate
the sister chromatids in each of the two cells. Once separated, each is
counted as a chromosome in its product cell.

Record counts per cell at these stable stages:
before replication; after replication; after division I; after division II.
Record the number of product cells separately from chromosome count per cell.
Include sketches so the adult can trace where every chromosome went.

For this first run, omit crossing over. Explain why this simplified run cannot
demonstrate every mechanism of genetic variation. No microscope observation,
human reproductive sample, organism collection or physical experiment is used.

### Day 4 — what the model leaves out (60 minutes)

10m retrieval; 15m explicit teaching; 15m guided comparison; 10m independent
application; 10m explanation.

Adult model: before homolog separation, nonsister chromatids of paired
homologs can exchange corresponding segments in crossing over. Draw a marked
segment exchange between one A chromatid and one a chromatid. Chromosome
number has not increased just because a segment's label changes.

Compare mitosis and meiosis: mitosis generally preserves chromosome number
in daughter nuclei, while this meiosis model reduces the number of sets.
They are not interchangeable descriptions of “a cell splitting.”

Independent application: a model cell starts with three homologous pairs.
Predict chromosomes per final meiotic product, assuming normal segregation.
Explain your reasoning without merely copying the earlier numbers.

Limitations: drawings omit actual molecules, spindle dynamics, timing and many
regulatory processes. Four drawn products illustrate the divisions; they do
not establish that every organism produces four equivalent functional gametes.

### Day 5 — independent demonstration (60 minutes)

25m independent work; 20m adult conference; 15m correction or extension.

1. Explain homologous chromosomes versus sister chromatids using a sketch.
2. Complete the four-stage count table for the two-pair model.
3. Correct: “DNA replicates before both divisions, so the final cells keep
   the starting diploid number.”
4. Explain why a crossing-over segment does not add a chromosome.
5. A new model begins with five homologous pairs. Predict chromosomes in
   each final product after normal meiosis. Name one limitation of your model.

Keep first-attempt work distinct from corrections. Enlarged print, exact
read-aloud, a verbatim scribe and learner-directed tactile labels are allowed;
record assistance. Do not supply count answers during independent work.

## Delayed and unfamiliar-application check

Seven days later, use 10 minutes within the next week's allocation.
An unfamiliar organism model has diploid number 8. State chromosomes per cell
after replication, after division I and after division II. Explain why “more
DNA after replication” need not mean “more chromosomes.”

Extension after secure answers: two homologs from the same pair mistakenly
enter one first-division product. Explain why the ordinary equal-set assumption
no longer holds. No human diagnosis or personal inheritance inference follows.

## Bede guidance

Ask one targeted question: “Are you counting lines or centromeres?”;
“Which partners separate in this division?”; “Did any DNA replication happen
between these two stages?”; “Does each product have one member of every pair?”
On an active independent check, ask for the learner's attempt without giving
the completed count table. If a tool reveals answers, mark that work assisted.

## Adult-only key — remove before learner distribution

For the two-pair model:

| Stable stage | Cells represented | Chromosomes per cell | Chromatids per cell |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Before replication | 1 | 4 | 4 |
| After replication | 1 | 4 | 8 |
| After division I | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| After division II | 4 | 2 | 2 |

This table counts stable stages, not the transient whole-cell counts while
sister centromeres separate before cytokinesis. At the final stage the former
chromatids are independent, unduplicated chromosomes.

Day 1's single homologous pair: two chromosomes and two chromatids before
replication; two chromosomes and four chromatids after. The full cell always
has two homologous pairs before division I.

Day 2 alternative: A with b, and a with B. Each receives one member from each
pair. A and a together with no B/b fails that criterion.
Day 4: three chromosomes in each final product, assuming normal segregation.

Day 5: homologs correspond by gene locations and parental origin; sisters
are replicated copies of one chromosome, with possible later changes through
crossing over. DNA replicates once before division I, not again before II.
Crossing over exchanges segments without increasing centromere count.
Five pairs give five chromosomes per final product. Accept the stated model
limitations, not an unsupported claim that paper tokens establish real biology.

Delayed: 8, 4 and 4 chromosomes per cell respectively. Replication doubles
chromatid/DNA content but not the count of centromeres at that stage.
Extension: unequal homolog distribution invalidates the normal balanced-set
prediction; a product can receive both homologs of one pair while another
receives neither. Do not treat this classroom model as clinical guidance.

Assess Day 5 items on correct claim plus explanation, two points each.
Provisional progression requires at least 8/10 and correct distinction between
replication and chromosome number and between the two divisions. These are
draft instructional criteria, not validated mastery scores. Reteach a specific
misconception and use a fresh model; consult delayed evidence before claiming
retention.

## Adult delivery, safety and status

Homeschool/co-op: adult leads the brief models and checks drawings; learners
can explain their models to one another before individual assessment.
Charter/pod: teacher demonstration, paired paper investigation, individual
count table and oral explanation. Adapt grouping without making group output
evidence of each learner's independent understanding.

Estimated adult preparation 15m; direct modelling/checking 15m on Days 1, 4, 5,
10m on Day 2 and 30m on Day 3, plus 5m delayed scoring: 105m total. This is a
planning estimate, not observed staffing evidence. Learner allocation remains
300m, with the delayed check replacing 10m of next week's practice.

Default to drawn tokens, avoiding scissors and small loose pieces. Offer
breaks and accessible labels; never require color discrimination alone.
No biological samples, accounts or learner uploads are needed.
