# Biology investigation and safety plan

## Scope and responsibility

**Safety review required:** the [adoption review](ADOPTION-REVIEW.md) records procedure clarification requests under the owner-directed agentic review policy. These instructions are not a specialist safety certification; resolve applicable requests or exclude/replace unsafe activities before use. Adult supervision and site controls remain mandatory.

One90-minute investigation/data slot each week plus30minutes notebook analysis elsewhere in Biology's five hours. **Eighteen designated investigations** are listed below; other weeks use preparation, interpretation, safety, report revision or demonstrations. No full wet-laboratory facility is included.

Of the18: **6 bench/organism measurements** (weeks1,3,4,6,8,32), **3 field-observation studies** (20,24,30), and **9 explicit models/evidence investigations** (2,10,12,14,16,18,22,26,28). Physical indicator and germination activities have been excluded following agentic safety findings; their paper replacements do not count as bench work. Week32 repeats measurement. Prepared slides/image alternatives and accessibility substitutions may reduce actual hands-on work; record what occurred. Do not count model time or supplied data as organism experimentation.

Ninety minutes×18=27 scheduled investigation hours; the remaining18×90=27h are lab-related studio, **54h total scheduled**, not54h bench experimentation. Actual setup, observation and analysis minutes must be logged. A school must approve whether its lab-credit requirements are met, add facility-based investigations if necessary **by reallocating existing biology time or transparently revising workload**, and identify staffing/material costs.

## Before every investigation

Adult reads procedure and safety data for selected products, checks allergies/mobility/vision/access needs, confirms permission/site/weather, examines equipment and signs risk approval. Learner repeats the stop rule and cleanup plan. No lone unsupervised lab work, eating/drinking, tasting, touching face, or reuse of lab food as food. Wash hands and surfaces. Label all cups **NOT FOR CONSUMPTION**.

No unknown chemicals, flames, pressurized/closed fermentation, cleaning-product mixing, environmental microbe culturing, body fluids, live vertebrate experiments, dissection, foraging/edibility testing or wild-organism handling. No exact home locations, faces or protected-species coordinates in records. Local medical/emergency plans and adult training are institution-specific, not replaced by this document.

Adult alone uses cutting tools/heated water. Use plastic instead of glass when possible. If glass slides are used, adult checks edges and handles breakage with a proper sharps procedure. Eye protection is required for splash-risk work; gloves when a risk assessment/allergy/skin need indicates them, not as a substitute for handwashing. Stop for spills, injury, allergy symptoms, weather or uncertainty; notify adult, follow local emergency procedure and obtain appropriate care.

## Detailed starter procedures

### Investigation 1 — Leaf measurement, week 1

**Question:** how much do measured leaf lengths vary within one safe sample? **Materials:**5 adult-approved fallen non-irritating leaves, ruler/mm scale, paper, optional magnifier. **Risks:** allergy, sharp litter, plants/pesticide exposure, access/weather; adult gathers approved samples if field access unsafe.

1. Adult chooses safe permitted source and inspects leaves; do not collect restricted/unknown irritating specimens.
2. Define length as blade base-to-tip, excluding stalk. Record sample1–5, qualitative shape and length in cm to a consistent precision.
3. Remeasure each without looking at the first result; preserve both results.
4. Calculate mean/range; sketch two leaves; describe within-sample variation and measurement disagreement separately.
5. Return approved material or dispose in household waste as locally appropriate; wash hands.

**Check:** observation includes units and rule; inference does not claim regional/species representativeness. **Alternative:** safe grocery herb leaves with allergy review; tactile ruler/partner measurement; supplied photographs only if physical access cannot be arranged, labeled as such.

### Investigation 2 — Qualitative indicator evidence model, week 2

**Question:** what can repeated color descriptions support without calibration? **Materials:** paper or accessible text of the original example table below; no liquids, food, heat or chemical handling. **Status:** model/supplied-data activity, not an experiment. The former physical method is excluded because product strengths, preparation and volumes were insufficiently specified. An adult demonstration is not a bypass.

**Invented observations, not measured:** sample A: purple/purple; sample B: pink/pink; sample C: blue/blue-green in two hypothetical repeats. Read color words aloud or use labeled tactile tokens; color discrimination is not assessed. Copy both repeat descriptions, identify agreement and disagreement, and propose controls for a hypothetical future method (same light, volumes and indicator preparation). Explain why these observations cannot establish exact pH, sample identity or safety.

**Key:** A/B agree descriptively, C differs; none has a calibrated numerical pH or established acid/base identity. A color difference is evidence within the invented scenario, not evidence that a learner performed chemistry. End by identifying missing calibration/control evidence and filing paper materials. Physical work requires a separately authored and reviewed procedure, not this model.

### Investigation 3 — Plant-cell observation, week 3

**Question:** what structures can be resolved in this preparation? **Materials:** borrowed microscope, prepared plant slide OR adult-prepared onion epidermis in water, droppers, slides/coverslips, drawing paper. **Risks:** glass/cutting/onion irritation/equipment; adult cuts and prepares slides, preferably plastic-safe alternatives where suitable.

Start at lowest power; adult demonstrates focusing and carrying. Observe2–3fields; record magnification and actual visible boundaries. Sketch with scale/magnification and label only observed features, using “not resolved” where necessary. Compare with an attributed reference diagram. No stains, body specimens or cultures. Adult handles slide disposal/cleanup.

**Check:** no invented chloroplasts/nuclei; distinguish diagram from evidence. **Alternative:** prepared slide and camera projection controlled by trained adult; if only images are available, record image-analysis rather than microscope-operation competency.

### Investigation 4 — Potato osmosis, week 4

**Question:** how does salt treatment relate to tissue mass change? **Materials:**4similar adult-cut potato strips, balance reading at least0.1g, plain water, food salt, cups, measuring spoon/jug, paper towel, timer, eye protection for splash risk. **Risks:** blade, ingestion, spills; adult cuts, no food reuse.

1. Adult makes two replicates per treatment; if equipment permits use more. Label each sample.
2. Prepare and document plain water and a **5g salt per100mL water** classroom mixture; this is a recipe, not certified solution concentration. Equal volumes should cover equal-sized samples.
3. Blot consistently; record initial mass, dimensions, mixture, temperature, start time. Immerse30minutes.
4. While waiting, draw prediction/model, identify controlled variables and practice percentage calculation.
5. Remove, blot with the same method and weigh. Record actual values, including unexpected ones.
6. Calculate percent change; compare replicate values; interpret cautiously. A0.1g balance/small sample may not resolve a small change; repeat with longer supervised soak if appropriate, without inventing data.
7. Adult approves disposal and surface cleaning; no tasting.

**Check:** no causal certainty beyond controlled variables/sample; note timing/blotting/tissue variability. **Alternative:** adult-precut safe material/partner weighing; separately labeled authentic teacher dataset plus direct observation when a balance is unavailable. Dataset alone does not demonstrate weighing.

## Specialist/facility needs and supervision

A trained adult must teach microscope use and manage slides. Safe temperature control, balances, prepared slides and accessibility equipment may require a co-op/school lab visit. No chemical stockroom, sterile culture facility or live vertebrate facility is assumed. If local biology standards require dissection, chemical procedures or instrument access not supplied here, an approved specialist must provide an ethical/safe alternative or a properly equipped supervised session; do not improvise at home.

Each notebook records date, actual duration, objective, risk approval, method, samples, raw observations, calculations, conclusions, cleanup and **physical/field/model/image/supplied-data** designation. The adult checks real data before accepting a report. No claim that this plan alone satisfies any jurisdiction's science-credit or safety regulation.
