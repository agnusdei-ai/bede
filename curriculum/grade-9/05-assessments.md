# Assessment and portfolio toolkit

## Principles and grade decisions

Adults retain grading, placement and credit decisions. A rubric organizes evidence; it does not diagnose intelligence or issue a transcript. Use accommodations agreed before assessment, record which skill they support, and do not confuse writing speed with analytical quality.

Keep **unaided evidence** (paper problem, passage interpretation, oral explanation) alongside **revised/assisted work**. No generative assistance during placement or unaided checks. During a project, permitted assistance must be disclosed: tool/version/date, task, relevant prompt/output, what the learner accepted/rejected and how claims/code were verified. No student PII in an AI prompt. Spellcheck, text-to-speech or scribing may be access tools; document them separately from generated reasoning.

Suggested internal weighting, subject to local approval:

- Literature: revised writing40%, unaided passage work20%, seminar20%, reading/process portfolio20%.
- History: source/map work30%, synthesis writing30%, demonstrations25%, chronology/oral checks15%.
- Math: unaided reasoning checks50%, corrected practice20%, applications/oral explanation30%.
- Biology: investigations/notebook40%, concept/data checks35%, demonstrations25%.
- Latin: reading/parsing40%, retrieval/forms25%, oral/listening20%, revision/process15%.
- Studio: project/tests40%, explanation/verification30%, process/privacy20%, unaided tracing10%.

Average quarterly evidence within each category only after sufficient samples. An empty category is **missing evidence**, not an automatic zero or perfect score. Offer a supported reassessment after instruction. Do not award a lab designation when physical investigation requirements remain unmet.

## Readiness diagnostics — before week 1 or on its first day

### Reading/writing, 45–60m

Use a lawfully accessed 700–1,000-word selection from *Odyssey* I (adult marks the exact start/end). Learner reads/annotates, writes a150-word summary, identifies a claim about a character with supporting words, and writes250words explaining that claim. Ask two oral questions: “What did the passage explicitly say?” and “What might another reader infer differently?” Note decoding/access barriers separately from interpretation.

**Interpretation:** adequate summary preserves who did what and avoids invented events; evidence is relevant; explanation links words to claim. If passage length/access is a barrier, reread with audio or shorter equivalent and record support. This is placement information, not a pass/fail gate.

### Mathematics, 30m unaided +10m oral

1. −7+12. 2. 3/4+1/2. 3. 2/3÷4/5. 4. 15% of80.
5. Solve3x+5=20. 6. Evaluate2a²−b for a=−2,b=3.
7. A recipe uses3cups for8people: cups for20? 8. Slope through(0,2),(3,8)?
9. Write an equation for “twice a number, less4, is10.”
10. Explain why1/2+1/3 is not2/5.

**Key:** 1 5; 2 5/4; 3 5/6; 4 12; 5 x=5; 6 5; 7 7.5cups proportional assumption; 8 2; 9 2x−4=10; 10 denominator describes equal-sized parts, so use sixths:3/6+2/6=5/6. Score1 per correct result plus an independent reasoning note; never use the 10-point total alone. Fewer than7 secure items, especially fractions/equations, signals a diagnostic conference/bridge, not exclusion. Secure7–10 with explainable reasoning supports an Algebra start.

**Geometry-placement extension, 40m:** factor x²−5x+6; solve2x+y=7 and x−y=2; find roots of x²=9; compare f(x)=2x+1 with g(x)=x² at x=−1,0,2; explain domain in a distance model; solve x²−4x−5=0.

**Key:** (x−2)(x−3); x=3,y=1; ±3; f=−1,1,5 and g=1,0,4; real context may restrict inputs to nonnegative time/distance; roots5,−1. A qualified instructor reviews broad Algebra I evidence beyond these six examples; errors in one topic trigger targeted review. Do not declare Algebra I mastery from this small set alone.

### Science/safety, 20m

Learner reads a three-step procedure, labels “length5cm” observation vs “plant healthy” inference, identifies independent/dependent variables in a light/growth question, measures a safe object and states a spill response. Expected: record units, vary light while measuring growth and controlling other factors, stop/notify adult/avoid contact on spill. Any safety misunderstanding means reteaching before lab, not independent access.

### Language and computing, 15–20m

No prior Latin/Python expected. Ask learner to identify subject/object in “The girl carries water,” follow a three-card sequence, and trace “if length>5 label long, otherwise short” at length5. Expected subjectgirl/objectwater; equality falls in “otherwise.” Use findings to plan explicit instruction, not exclude beginners.

## Common 0–4 analytic scale

**4** independent, accurate and well justified, including limitations; **3** sound evidence with minor omissions; **2** partial understanding requiring prompts; **1** major confusion with little usable evidence; **0** no assessable evidence yet. Score dimensions separately; a total does not erase an important weakness. Record accommodations and assistance.

### Writing rubric — five dimensions, /20

1. **Claim:** precise contestable interpretation, not a topic label.
2. **Evidence/context:** accurate quotations/paraphrases, book/scene references and attribution.
3. **Reasoning:** explains evidence, handles contrary evidence fairly.
4. **Organization/style:** logical paragraphs, transitions, readable sentences.
5. **Revision/integrity:** meaningful revision and honest source/tool disclosure.

At3: clear claim, relevant evidence, mostly connected explanation, coherent structure and documented revision. At2: broad claim, thin or weakly contextualized evidence, summary replacing analysis or superficial editing. At4: well-delimited interpretation, exact context and a counterreading that changes/refines the argument. Do not give polished generated prose4 when the learner cannot explain it.

### Seminar rubric — four dimensions, /16

Preparation/textual references; responsive listening; reasoning/questioning; equitable contribution. A3 uses the text and accurately responds to another idea without dominating. A4 helps clarify a genuine disagreement and invites participation. A2 states views but rarely uses evidence or follows others. An accessible written/oral response can demonstrate preparation/reasoning; participation format is adapted without grading disability-related speech traits.

### Math reasoning rubric — four dimensions, /16

Representation/model; valid steps; explanation/verification; units/domain/interpretation. A3 has a defensible method, largely accurate work and meaningful check. A4 compares methods or explains a boundary/limitation. A2 may obtain a correct number with weak explanation or an unexamined assumption. A1 applies an unrelated rule. Grade arithmetic accuracy separately when it obscures otherwise valid reasoning.

### Lab notebook/report rubric — five dimensions, /20

Question/variables; safe reproducible method; actual data/units; analysis/uncertainty; evidence-bounded conclusion/reflection. A3 gives enough procedure detail to repeat, honest data and an appropriately limited conclusion. A4 improves a control or sampling design and explains its effect. A2 omits units/replicates or overstates a small sample. A0 in data means no actual record; supplied backup data must be labeled, not passed off as collected. Unsafe practice is a stop-and-reteach event regardless of total score.

### Code/project rubric — five dimensions, /20

Decomposition/logic; functioning code and reproducible instructions; tests/error analysis; provenance/privacy; oral explanation/limitations. A3 can run the baseline, test edge cases and explain each branch. A4 uses meaningful held-out cases, discusses trade-offs and documents reproducibility. A2 runs copied code without a clear account of behavior. An attractive interface alone supplies little evidence.

## First-quarter assessment examples

### Week 4 integrated checkpoint

- Literature: 400–500-word hospitality argument using passages actually read from books I–VIII, two textual references and one contrary detail from the reading log; writing rubric. Document 04's 300m weekly agenda includes this work; reduce writing length if needed without adding homework or requiring unabridged completion.
- History:400-word Egypt/Nubia–Mesopotamia comparison of one named practice, with dated examples and limitations.
- Math,20–25m: solve2(x−3)=10; solve−3x≥12; graph y=2x+1 for x=−1,0,2; explain why2x+3=2x+5 has no solution; model a $20 budget with $5fixed cost and $3peritem.
- Biology: actual potato-osmosis report and oral explanation of percent change and limits.
- Latin: translate *puellae fābulās amant* and *aquam portāmus*; parse *puellīs* in *nauta puellīs fābulam nārrat*.
- Studio: trace the included baseline on a missing field, a needle-like leaf and a compound blade; explain why it does not identify a species.

**Math key:** x=8; x≤−4; points(−1,−1),(0,1),(2,5); contradiction3=5;5+3n≤20,n whole/nonnegative,max5. Graph prompt previews slope formally studied later; assess coordinate substitution now, not unintroduced slope vocabulary.

**Latin key:** girls like stories; we carry water; *puellīs* dative plural indirect object, “to the girls.” Studio: unknown when a required feature is absent, needle-like category for needle trait, compound category when supplied.

### Week 6 data check, 20m

Practice-only yeast records: two matched cups at the same temperature, one with sugar and one without; foam heights at10minutes3cm vs1cm. Ask for independent variable, dependent proxy, two controls, one reason this does not directly measure total respiration.

**Guidance:** sugar availability; foam height as imperfect gas proxy; same yeast/water/temperature/time/cup geometry; bubbles depend on mixing and foam stability and are not direct CO₂ volume or total respiration. No causal generalization beyond the limited setup without repeats. Teacher accepts better controlled designs and notes actual lab results may differ.

### Week 9 quarter demonstration — sample paper/oral bank

**Literature,45m unaided:** adult marks a short scene from *Odyssey* XVII or XVIII already read; learner argues how identity, hospitality or trust is developed, using two details, then answers “What detail could complicate your interpretation?” No single mandated thesis. A good answer distinguishes a character's conduct from narrator certainty. Do not assess later recognition scenes before weeks10–12.

**History,40m:** order2500BCE,1200BCE,500BCE,200CE; locate Nile/Indus; explain one difference between the already-read *Odyssey* I representation of a hospitality norm and evidence of actual practice; compare one institution/practice from two Q1 regions using the replacement source-log evidence. Use document 04's five-entry portfolio: Odyssey norm, UNG Egypt/Kush, exchange, Herodotus reported tribute, and Indus/South Asia. Key: order as listed; map accuracy; represented norm versus practice. Identify Homeric epic/uncertain archaic composition and Butler translation, cite Telemachus feeding the guest before questioning, and explain why literary welcome cannot establish universal conduct. For Herodotus III.89–90 distinguish ancient narrator, Macaulay translation and modern UNG interpretation; a tribute list does not prove consent or universal collection. Require provenance, a supported claim and a limit, scored with unchanged chronology/geography, evidence/context, comparison and limitations dimensions (/16). No law-code feature or Behistun image is assigned. Fictional grain rules never count as historical sources. Comparison needs context, not cultural ranking. Source selections/rights exclusions are pinned in the replacement register; independent review is not claimed complete.

**Math,45m:** (1)3(2x−1)=4x+9; (2)−2(x+1)<6; (3) graph y=−x+3 and state intercepts; (4) slope through(1,2),(4,8); (5) $8fixed plus$2pervisit, budget$22: maximum visits; (6) explain why a line's intercept must be interpreted with units.

**Key:**1 x=6;2 x>−4;3 y-intercept(0,3),x-intercept(3,0);4 2;5 7whole visits;6 intercept is output when input0, with model-dependent units and possibly outside useful domain. Award reasoning independently; review negative-division misconception.

**Biology,40m:** label a cell drawing from actual observed structures; interpret leaf-data mean/range; distinguish respiration/photosynthesis by inputs/outputs at overview level; critique “six dividing cells among30 proves20% of all plant cells are dividing.”

**Key:** plant cell wall may be observed, organelles not necessarily; calculations follow actual table; respiration releases usable energy from organic molecules while photosynthesis captures light into chemical energy;6/30=20% **of sampled cells under those observations**, not all plants/all times. Sampling/preparation affect count; no diagnostic use.

**Latin,25m:** read *Puella in īnsulā est. Nauta aquam portat. Puellae fābulās amant. Servus dōnum portat.* Translate and parse *īnsulā*, *puellae*, *dōnum*. Adult pre-teaches/retrieves *servus* as enslaved person in historical context, never celebrates slavery.

**Key:** girl is on island; sailor carries water; girls like stories; enslaved person carries a gift. *īnsulā* ablative singular after stationary *in*; *puellae* nominative plural, supported by *amant*; *dōnum* accusative singular neuter here though nominative has same form. Document 04's nine-sentence replacement also accepts sentence4's dative-recipient or justified genitive-possessor readings in both translation and parsing; do not impose a unique reading on that uncontextualized sentence. Missing keyboard macrons are accommodated unless vowel quantity is the stated objective; supplied models retain the correct quantities.

**Studio,20m:** run frozen tests, predict an unseen record, explain one mistaken classification and one limit; no code generation during oral explanation. Student may consult their own code for the demonstration, then trace a short branch unaided.

## Quarterly demonstrations after Q1

- **Q2/week18:** dramatic essay and source panel; systems/factoring check; inheritance/selection model critique; Latin connected reading; project version2 error audit.
- **Q3/week27:** epic comparison, comparative atlas, rational/radical reasoning, organism/ecology design critique, Latin reading, reproducible project version3.
- **Q4/weeks35–36:** unfamiliar passage/source, cumulative Algebra reasoning, safe biology practical/data defense, Latin parsing, final code/oral defense and next-placement conference.

These later assessments are **specifications, not fully written exams or answer keys**. Adult authors/selects items aligned to taught content before the quarter; avoid high-stakes use of unseen unreviewed generated tests. Source alignment is now explicit: Q2 uses H18/M18/B18; Q3 uses H27/M27/B27; Q4 uses H34–H36/M34–M36/B34–B36 in the [replacement register](resources/REPLACEMENT-REGISTER.md). The unfamiliar history selection is Macaulay's Herodotus III.91 only; preteach provenance, not its answer. All math checks here are original, not externally licensed exercise-bank adaptations. Biology checks use original notes and actual or explicitly labeled example data; no removed textbook figure or publisher answer key is required.

## Portfolio checklist and record template

At each quarter retain:

- One dated unaided writing sample, draft/revised argument and revision memo.
- Seminar preparation plus adult observation; historical map/timeline/source comparison.
- Unaided math check, corrections and one oral/modeling record.
- Biology procedures/risk approvals, actual tables, graphs, observation drawings and limitations; ledger states physical/model/supplied-data status.
- Latin reading/forms sample with teacher corrections.
- Code snapshot, data card, tests including failed cases, assistance log and oral-defense note.
- PE/arts log and demonstration.
- Adult feedback, accommodations, learner reflection and next plan.

**Record fields:** date | course/objective | artifact | independent/assisted/access support | rubric dimensions | feedback | reassessment | credit/time allocation. Keep records securely under family/school policy, minimize identifiers and do not publish learner work without appropriate permission. This is a template, not a new student-data system.
