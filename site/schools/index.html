A LEARNING OPERATING SYSTEM, NOT AN AUTONOMOUS TEACHER

From evidence to a teacher's next move

Choose a meaningful goal

The educator names the text, academic objective, and kind of explanation or application that would show understanding.

Review before acting

In the current pilot, review synthetic evidence and approve the instructional plan. The teacher—not Bede—delivers the lesson and decides the follow-up.

Ask for transfer

Use a new example, written explanation, or practical problem to see whether a learner can use an idea beyond the original exercise. This is educator-led pedagogy, not an automated pilot feature.

A classroom example: defend an interpretation

After a literature discussion, ask students to support an interpretation with a passage, consider a competing reading, and revise a short response. Later, use an unfamiliar passage to see whether the reasoning carries over. This is an illustrative teacher-led activity, not a report of pilot results.

What to evaluate: agree in advance on a baseline task, a comparable later task, and a delayed application in a new context. Have educators review the work and any additional support needed. More focused decisions and feedback are the intended benefits; accelerated outcomes have not yet been established by an independent study.

Less preparation. Clear options. Your decision.

Workflow direction to evaluate: start from the existing lesson and a small amount of evidence; prepare a concise educator brief and a few useful options; verify draft materials; let the teacher approve, edit, reject, or defer; then teach and review the application. Paper-first lessons, recitation, narration, and discussion remain human-led. Proposed brief formats, printable bundles, and verification checks are design goals—not claims that the current pilot implements every artifact or check.

Use the right device for the task: mobile for a brief review where supported, a tablet for classroom reference, and a desktop for planning. Device access depends on the school's qualified environment, permissions, and network. This does not enable student mobile tutoring in the current teacher-only pilot or promise offline operation.

Questions to answer before a meeting

Your leadership and IT teams can use these questions to prepare a clear request for pricing.

  1. How many campuses? List the physical school locations you want to include.
  2. How many learners? Estimate enrollment at each campus for the next 12 months.
  3. What equipment is available? Confirm whether you will need a Mac mini and who will manage it.
  4. Which integrations? Identify any student information or learning management systems Bede needs to work with.
  5. What support and timeline? Decide what teacher onboarding, response times, and evaluation dates matter.

Why this matters: The software price alone is not the full implementation budget. We will confirm hardware, expected simultaneous use, integration work, and support terms before agreeing to a school rollout.

What teachers can expect

In the current pilot, Bede supports synthetic evidence review, teacher-approved instructional planning, and progress review. Teachers deliver instruction and choose follow-up actions. Direct student tutoring belongs only to a separately qualified deployment. Bede does not replace educators, administer official DIBELS assessments, or grade children on character or faith.

Unlimited enrolled learner accounts do not mean unlimited students can use the tutor at once. The number of simultaneous sessions depends on the tested equipment and setup.

Common questions

Does the $2,600 founding-campus price cover all of our schools?

No. The price covers unlimited enrolled learner accounts at one licensed physical campus. Each additional campus needs its own license. Simultaneous usage depends on qualified hardware capacity.

Can we change the way we are billed during the year?

Yes, if both parties agree in writing. Schools can switch between per-learner and campus pricing by a mutually agreed written amendment that specifies the effective date. The 12-month commitment remains unless the agreement says otherwise.

Is the equipment included in the software price?

No. Mac mini hardware, applicable taxes, custom school-system integrations, onsite services, and custom infrastructure are priced separately. We will confirm capacity and costs before a school deployment.

How does Bede support teachers?

In the current pilot, Bede supports synthetic assessment review, Data Day evidence, teacher-approved instructional plans, and progress review. Teachers deliver instruction; Bede does not tutor learners directly in this pilot. Student tutoring requires separate qualification. Teachers choose the curriculum and next steps.

Is the $950 evaluation the same as the current Valor pilot?

No. The $950 evaluation is a separate paid offer, proposed for 6 to 8 weeks and about 15 to 25 learners, subject to a signed scope and deployment qualification before any real learner data or direct tutoring. The current invite-only Valor pilot is free, manually approved, teacher-directed, and uses synthetic data only; it does not tutor learners directly.

Can Bede administer DIBELS or grade a student's character?

No. Bede does not administer or score DIBELS assessments. Educators can enter independently administered results as reported. Bede records academic work, not judgments about a student's faith or character.

Where is student information stored?

For a self-hosted deployment, student data is kept on the school's equipment under the agreed security and data-handling setup. Schools should review the data disclosures, backup plan, and access controls before signing.
