## Week 1: Sequence and inference

Lesson: `g8-year-literature-w01` · Draft version 1

### Learning focus

Apply and explain sequence and inference using the specified independent and transfer tasks.

### Before starting

Retell a short passage in sequence and distinguish a stated event from an inference.

### Explicit Teaching

Suggested time: 12 minutes.

Reading assignment: Alice, chapter 1. Spread reading over four sittings; pause for oral or written narration before analysis. Narration selects events in order; inference adds a claim that must fit a detail. Keep a two-column event/inference record and mark uncertainty. Access to the full lawful text is essential; a synopsis or this question is not a substitute.
Entry check: ask the learner to demonstrate the stated prerequisite with a fresh example. If missing, teach that skill first; do not treat age or an earlier week number as evidence of readiness.

Paper, pencil or accessible response tool

Adult packet kept separate from independent work

Adult-supplied lawful complete editions: Lewis Carroll, Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (1865, 12 chapters); Robert Louis Stevenson, Treasure Island (1883, 34 chapters); Jane Austen, Persuasion (1818, 24 chapters). No text or modern notes ingested.

Adult checks depictions of violence, alcohol, class, gender and historical language; give context, private response routes and a reviewed alternative if unsuitable.
