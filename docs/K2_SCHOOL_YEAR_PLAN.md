# K-2 school year: numbers, phonics and maps

A 36-week plan for each grade, with one skill a week in each area. Each skill is practised in Bede's five-minute Watch, Together, Your turn round, five days a week. English phonics carries a sight-word strand, and Spanish (Mexico) has its own native phonics year. This is a plan: none of the new skills are built yet. Data file: [`K2_SCHOOL_YEAR_PLAN.csv`](K2_SCHOOL_YEAR_PLAN.csv) (432 rows: numbers, English phonics, maps and Spanish phonics for every week).

## How to read it

Each skill carries a status mark:

| Mark | Meaning | Count |
|---|---|---|
| `B` | A practice round for this skill exists on `main` today. | 51 |
| `E` | Bede already has this kind of skill or question, but it needs new items or a new question type. | 146 |
| `N` | A new skill. | 235 |

Even `B` phonics weeks need more words. A week of daily rounds uses about 30 word slots, and each phonics area has only about 10 words today.

"One skill a week" is the default pace. Phonics moves on only when the week's skill is mastered; otherwise the child repeats it with new words while numbers and maps keep going. Each day's round also mixes in a few items from earlier weeks.

## Where the order comes from

- **Numbers** follow the unit order of Illustrative Mathematics K-5, first edition: 8 units in K and grade 1, 9 in grade 2 ([kindergarten](https://im.kendallhunt.com/K5/teachers/kindergarten/course-guide/scope-and-sequence.html), [grade 1](https://im.kendallhunt.com/K5/teachers/grade-1/course-guide/scope-and-sequence.html), [grade 2](https://im.kendallhunt.com/K5/teachers/grade-2/course-guide/scope-and-sequence.html)). That edition allows commercial use under CC BY 4.0 with the credit line "Based on IM® K–12 Math by Illustrative Mathematics®, CC BY 4.0" ([IM licensing](https://illustrativemathematics.org/site-ip-content/)). IM's v.360 edition is non-commercial only, so don't use it. Bede's items are still generated by its own code.
- **English phonics** follows the usual simple-to-complex order: sounds by ear, letter sounds, short vowels, digraphs and blends, silent e, vowel teams, r-controlled vowels, syllables and word endings ([UFLI scope and sequence](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/UFLI-Scope2.pdf); [IES foundational skills guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/wwc_found_reading_summary_051517.pdf)). Every word list is written by Bede. Core Knowledge Language Arts is CC BY-NC-SA, which rules out commercial use ([Core Knowledge terms](https://www.coreknowledge.org/terms-of-use/)).
- **Spanish phonics** follows the syllabic order used in Mexico: vowels, then consonant-vowel syllables, then context-dependent spellings (c/qu, g/gu), then blends, diphthongs and accent marks.
- **Maps** extend Bede's 9 map skills. They move from real places to photos, tilted views and then maps from above ([step-by-step map progression](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1350293X.2024.2322528)). Outlines can come from Natural Earth, which is public domain and free for commercial use with no credit needed ([Natural Earth terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)).

## Research base

The full review is in [`K2_DYSLEXIA_RESEARCH.md`](K2_DYSLEXIA_RESEARCH.md). How mastery is scored is in [`K2_PRACTICE_RESEARCH.md`](K2_PRACTICE_RESEARCH.md). In short:

- Systematic phonics is the only dyslexia approach with a confirmed effect in randomised trials, and it works best when it starts in K or grade 1 ([Galuschka et al., 2014](https://dx.plos.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089900); [Ehri et al., 2001](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543071003393)).
- In Spanish, dyslexia shows up mainly as slow reading rather than errors, so the Spanish year needs a fluency strand ([IDA definition](https://dyslexiaida.org/definition-of-dyslexia/); [Serrano and Defior, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18483868/)).
- Phonics apps only work well when an adult is involved ([McTigue et al., 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1238280)). Keep the parent in the "Together" step.
- Reading and maths difficulties often go together, so number work never depends on the child's reading. Story problems are read aloud ([Frontiers in Psychology, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1664437/full)).
- Bede never labels a child. When practice shows risk signs, it can suggest that the parent talk to a reading specialist.

## Spanish phonics (Mexico)

Spanish is taught as its own native phonics year, not as a translation of the English one. Grade names follow Mexico: K is 3° de preescolar. A daily one-minute reread of known syllables and words starts in late K, because speed is the main warning sign in Spanish. Spanish frequent words are taught inside phonics; Spanish spelling is regular enough that no separate memorisation list is needed. Voices: Bede's Piper `es_MX` voices.

This is in line with common practice lists ([Fry list overview](https://www.wonsterwords.com/resources/fry-sight-words); [sight-word benchmarks](https://readflare.com/articles/reading-at-home/sight-words-flash-cards)). The list is written by Bede, not copied from Fry or Dolch.
- **Words follow phonics.** A regular word (such as "can" or "it") enters the strand only after its sounds have been taught. Most high-frequency words are regular ([Reading Psychology, 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02702711.2024.2379255)).
- **Heart words are learned through sounds, not shape.** For an irregular word such as "said", the child says the sounds, finds the tricky part and spells it aloud. If the child reads it as spelled, Bede corrects the pronunciation. Both methods beat look-and-say ([2022 kindergarten trial](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10888438.2022.2077653); [2026 Year 1 trial](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10888438.2026.2671171)).
- **Review:** the 10 "Sight-word review" weeks cover every word so far, and daily rounds mix in a few older words.

## Any country

Any country works; the default is the United States, and Spanish defaults to Mexico. The parent picks the country, and only these skills change:

Bede never asks for a child's real address. Kindergarten "Home, street and town" uses a made-up town.

## One open choice

**Timing reading speed.** Bede's guide says never to time a child. The research says speed is the main warning sign in Spanish, and timed practice is well supported in maths. One option that keeps the promise to the child is for Bede to record reading speed silently and show it only to the parent, with no timer on screen. Should Bede do this?
