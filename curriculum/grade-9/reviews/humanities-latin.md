## Latin findings

### T1 — Required correction: model-text vowel quantity is inconsistent with its chosen source

**References:** 04 lines 72, 134–142, 323–335, 513–521, 673–693; D’Ooge §§12.3, 22, Lesson VII vocabulary, §129.

The authored teaching text consistently uses *insula/in insulā* rather than **īnsula/in īnsulā**, and *narrō/narrat/narrāmus* rather than D’Ooge’s **nārrō/nārrat/nārrāmus**. D’Ooge explicitly marks long vowels, gives *īn´sula*, and its rule §12.3 identifies length before *ns*. This is not merely a learner’s missing keyboard mark: the instructor model omits lexical quantities while claiming macrons teach vowel length. Endings such as *-ā, -ās, -ārum, -īs* are otherwise modeled correctly.

**Closure:** normalize the selected teaching convention across vocabulary, passages and keys; audit remaining quantities against the adopted source. Retain the sensible no-keyboard-macron penalty rule (04 line 695), but distinguish accessibility tolerance from correctness of the supplied model.

### T2 — Required correction: quarter sentence 4 admits taught genitive alternatives

**References:** 04 lines 684, 691, 693, 695; D’Ooge §§38, 42, 45.

*Amīcus puellae fābulam narrat* supports the intended “The friend tells the girl a story” (*puellae*, dative recipient), but equally supports **“The friend tells the girl’s story”** (*puellae*, genitive modifying *fābulam*), or **“The girl’s friend tells a story”** (genitive modifying *amīcus*). The prompt supplies no context eliminating them. The parsing key gives only dative. The blanket alternative-reading allowance at line 695 is helpful but should be instantiated here; nominative plural is excluded as the clause’s ordinary subject by singular *narrat*, not genitive singular.

**Closure:** explicitly accept justified genitive readings in both translation and parsing scoring, or provide context disambiguating recipient versus possessor. Do not teach that word order alone excludes the genitive. F6 already models honest ambiguity handling well.

### T3 — Required correction: bound/contextualize D’Ooge’s introductory historical assertions

**References:** 04 line 72 (“pronunciation/introduction”); 02 line 49; actual D’Ooge “To the Student,” “The Spread of Latin,” “Rome and the Modern World.”

The selected introduction says Rome ruled the “whole ancient world,” calls Latin the “only light of learning” through “dark ages,” and presents cultural hierarchies and mind-strengthening claims as facts. These are historically sweeping advocacy, not safe neutral background for the curriculum’s substantive non-Western study. The local warning that historical framing needs review does not itself supply a correction.

**Closure:** restrict input to identified pronunciation/grammar sections or supply an explicit contextual note rejecting universal-world/only-learning claims and distinguishing early twentieth-century textbook advocacy from evidence. Retain original grammar exercises rather than importing the book’s coercive or hierarchical social framing wholesale.

Additional independent checks:

- T3 permits *aquam puella portat* as well as supplied order; T16 *aquam portō* encodes first singular without a separate pronoun. Latin has no article: a/the alternatives are rightly accepted.
- T9/T13 distinguish genitive/dative singular *puellae* from nominative plural; *puellīs* is dative or ablative plural, with context/preposition determining the function.
- F5 *rosās puellārum portāmus*: accusative plural roses, genitive plural possessor, first plural verb. F6 *fābulam nautae amat*: intended genitive, with a justified dative-of-interest alternative correctly acknowledged.
- G2 *nautae sumus*: predicate noun nominative plural; no direct object. G1 can be existential “there are sailors,” not only “they are sailors”; its existing context-dependent wording should not penalize that natural rendering.
- S5 *amīcīs fābulās narrāmus*: dative plural friends and accusative plural stories; restore lexical *nā-*. *Servī* can be genitive singular or nominative plural in the assessed scope.
- N5 *puella dōna amīcī portat*: gifts plural object, singular friend as possessor. *Dōna est* intended as “gifts are” requires *sunt*; neuter plural is not singular because it ends in *-a*.
- Document 05’s short quarter key correctly makes *dōnum* accusative after *portat* despite identical nominative shape. Reading/parsing assessment stays inside taught present verbs, two declensions and governed prepositions; no adjectives, motion-into, passive or past tenses are smuggled into Q1.
