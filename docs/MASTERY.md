# Mastery: the foundations

*Read this first. It is short on purpose.*

Bede is built for learners outside a school system — homeschools, pods,
families teaching several children at once. That setting has no bell
curve, no class rank, and no reason to invent one. What it needs instead
is an honest answer to a plain question: **can this child do this, yet?**

Everything else in Bede follows from how that question is answered.

---

## 1. What mastery means here

Mastery is **demonstrated ability on one specific skill**, judged against
what the task actually asked for.

Not a grade. Not a percentage. Not a position relative to another child,
or to what a child that age "should" be doing, or even to how the same
child did last week. Those are all comparisons, and a comparison answers
a different question than the one a teaching parent is asking.

The unit matters. Bede does not track "maths" — it tracks *divides
fractions*, *finds slope as a rate of change*, *reads multisyllable
words*. A child is rarely good or bad at a subject; they are secure on
some of its skills and not yet on others, and only the smaller unit tells
you where to spend Tuesday morning.

---

## 2. Two instruments, and why you need both

This is the one idea worth understanding properly. Bede measures in two
completely different ways, and they answer different questions.

| | **The work ledger** | **The mastery estimate** |
|---|---|---|
| Holds | events — what was done | an inference — what they can probably do |
| Reads | "on 12 March, a long-division task was finished unaided" | "0.78 probability this skill is secure" |
| True from | the first entry | only after enough evidence |
| Answers | *what has my child actually done?* | *what can my child probably do?* |

**The ledger is true immediately.** It records events, so entry one is as
honest as entry two hundred. Nothing about a short history makes it
misleading.

**The estimate needs history.** It is a statistical claim, and a claim
built from four observations is a guess wearing a number. Bede says so
rather than hiding it: each area holds back its picture until it has
enough — two assessed narrations for writing, three for reading and
phonics, five for maths — and until then the card says it is still
getting to know your learner.

**In your first weeks, trust the ledger.** Look at what was finished and
how much help it took. That is real information from day one. The mastery
picture will fill in, and it is worth waiting for rather than believing
early.

---

## 3. How much help it took is the signal

Every completed piece of work records one of three things: done **on
their own**, **after a nudge**, or **worked through together**.

This is the most useful number on the page and the most commonly
misread. It describes **what the work needed**, not what your child is
capable of. All real teaching involves help. A skill that needed working
through together in March and is going unaided by May has moved — and
that movement is visible long before any probability shifts.

Bede also notes, where it saw enough to say, whether a piece of work was
**one to show**, whether the child **went further** than what was asked,
whether they brought **their own idea**, and whether it **came easily**
now. Only the notable end of each scale is ever shown. Ordinary, careful,
correct work earns no note — because putting "adequate" on screen would
read as a mark against it, and it isn't one.

A blank is not a low score. The card always tells you how much Bede
noted and how much went by without notes, so the two can never be
confused.

---

## 4. What Bede will not do

These are not preferences. They are the boundaries the whole design is
built inside, and they are enforced in code and pinned by tests.

- **It will not rank your children.** The pod view is organised by skill,
  never by child, with no per-child total anywhere — because one number
  standing for a child is all it takes to sort them. It shows who has
  done a piece of work so you can ask one child to show another. That is
  a roster, and it is deliberately not a league table.

- **It will not average anything.** You get counts and distributions. A
  mean over a three-point scale invents precision the scale doesn't
  carry, and reads as a grade.

- **It will not measure aptitude.** Bede reports what a child has and has
  not mastered. How clever they are is not a claim this software makes.

- **It will not diagnose.** If you tell Bede what helps your child, it
  acts on it and never asks why. Dyslexia, attention, anything of that
  kind is for a qualified evaluator, and Bede will not name, guess at, or
  imply one — to you or to your child.

- **It will not hurry a child.** Bede never times them, never mentions
  pace to them, and never treats working deliberately as working badly. A
  child who feels raced does worse work and enjoys it less.

- **It will not score their faith.** Bede prays with your family and
  teaches the subjects you choose. A child's spiritual life is not a
  metric, will not become one, and no counter exists for it anywhere.

- **It will not judge your child, only their work.** "That narration was
  one to show" is something Bede saw. "That child is exemplary" is a
  claim about a person, and it has no standing to make it.

---

## 5. Your part and Bede's part

**You teach.** Bede is a guide who sits alongside your child, works
through the material with them Socratically, and afterwards tells you
plainly what it saw — the way one adult describes a piece of work to the
adult responsible for it.

Nothing Bede records is a grade, a pass, a level, or a verdict on whether
your child is doing well enough. That judgment is yours, and Bede is
built to inform it rather than replace it. The constitution states the
order plainly: God and objective truth, then **the parent as the child's
primary educator**, then the dignity and developing conscience of the
child.

Concretely, this means Bede orders a day but never chooses one. The
planner returns your own subject list rearranged, with a reason for each
move — never a subject added, dropped, or shortened. Faith subjects stay
exactly where you put them.

---

## 6. Where you set the direction

Four fields do most of the work, all optional, all per child:

- **Subjects and grade** — what this child is doing, and at what stage.
- **What we're already covering** (`current_unit`, `lesson_focus`) — the
  book or unit you have going, so Bede anchors on your material rather
  than only its own.
- **Pick up where we left off** — your own note of exactly where a lesson
  stopped. This **outranks** Bede's own memory of the session when they
  disagree, because you were there.
- **What helps this child** — anything you know makes a lesson land
  better. It changes how a lesson is delivered, never what is taught or
  the standard it is held to.

---

## 7. When life interrupts

It will. The design assumes it.

**Bede does not lose your place.** At the end of every session it writes
a short note of where each subject actually stopped and reads it back
next time. After a fortnight the wording softens from "last time" to "a
while back" — it fades, it never deletes. A family that lapses for a
month does not come back to a blank slate.

**Travel gets a wider window.** Mastery is looked at over a rolling four
weeks by default. Turn on travel mode and that widens to as much as six,
so weeks away don't read as a stall.

**The window bounds the looking, never the child's work.** There is no
deadline on a person here. Nothing resets, nothing rolls over, and no
"velocity" is computed across cycles.

**And if a subject quietly stops happening**, the Progress page will tell
you — when each subject on the plan was last actually taught. That is a
fact about your schedule, not about your child. A subject can go untaught
because of the hour it sits at, the book, or a hard fortnight. You know
which. Bede doesn't, and won't pretend to.

---

## 8. What "it's working" looks like

In the first month, look for:

- Work getting finished, and the **help level dropping** on skills worked
  repeatedly.
- A **Math Skill Growth** section in the session summary showing real
  movement within that sitting.
- Subjects on the plan actually being taught.

After a term, look for:

- Skills moving from **Not started** through **Developing** to
  **Mastered** — the words the Progress page itself uses.
- Your chosen term topics showing movement inside the current cycle.
- Work occasionally going **further than what was asked** — the signal
  that a child is doing more than complying.

If you see none of that after a few honest weeks, the answer is usually
in the schedule rather than the child. Start there.
