## Core shelf and bounded reading

### Literature

- **Homer, Odyssey**, Samuel Butler prose translation, Gutenberg [1727](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1727). Target 24 books in weeks 1–12, averaging two books/week with review weeks; the supported excerpt pathway is not unabridged completion. Most weeks 30–50 pages depending on edition; use numbered books, not unstable page numbers. Butler uses **Ulysses = Odysseus, Minerva = Athena, Jove/Jupiter = Zeus, Neptune = Poseidon**. Lessons use the Greek names; either naming convention is acceptable with consistent identification. Assign the numbered epic narrative, not the translator's prefaces. Butler's young-woman authorship and Trapani-location hypotheses are his interpretive proposals, not established historical facts or evidence of a verified voyage route.
- **Sophocles, Antigone**, Francis Storr translation in *Oedipus Trilogy*, Gutenberg [31](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/31). Read only Antigone, by scene/speaker cues, weeks 14–18. Adult previews death, suicide, family conflict and authority.
- **Confucius, Analects**, James Legge, Gutenberg [3330](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3330). Selected short sayings, books I, II, IV, XII, XV; no requirement to read the entire work.
- **Bhagavad-Gita**, Edwin Arnold verse translation, Gutenberg [2388](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2388). Chapters II and III selected passages in weeks 22–23, contextual literary comparison; teacher explains this Victorian rendering and a living tradition cannot be reduced to a slogan.
- **Virgil, Aeneid**, John Dryden translation, Gutenberg [228](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/228). Selected books I, II, IV, VI and XII, weeks 26–31; difficult verse and violence preview required. Adult may read aloud or use a licensed accessible prose alternative, retaining passage references and analytical objectives.

Budget 120m/week assigned literary reading inside Literature's 300m, not additional homework. Weeks 1–4 use four 30m reading/annotation blocks on days 1–4; day 5 has no additional assigned reading block. Document 04 pins minimum analytical passages and the remaining discussion/writing minutes. Later weeks state their own bounded allocations. Reduce excerpt length or use supported audio when historical English blocks access; retain the minimum passage evidence, record actual reading versus adult-narrated plot, and reduce writing length if needed without adding hours. Prioritize accurate reading of the core rather than adding whole epics as “enrichment.” Optional extensions replace other reading minutes: a library-held modern translation of one core text, or an additional ancient narrative selected with educator guidance. No unlicensed scans.

Text alternatives: for Antigone's distressing scenes, offer a teacher-written plot outline plus carefully selected debate passages, with an equivalent authority/evidence essay. For difficult epics, combine short original excerpts, adult narration of omitted plot and oral analysis; record adaptations rather than claiming complete unabridged reading. Do not substitute a film alone for reading evidence.

### Latin

**Benjamin L. D'Ooge, Latin for Beginners**, Gutenberg [18251](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/18251). Full-year default pathway uses selected pronunciation/grammar topics with original course exercises here; do not assign the historical textbook cover-to-cover. Week 1 adult input is bounded to §§1–17 (alphabet, sounds, syllables, quantity and accent); exclude “To the Student,” “The Spread of Latin,” and “Rome and the Modern World.” Those introductory essays contain early twentieth-century advocacy: Rome did not rule the whole ancient world, Latin was not the world's only medium of learning, and cultural hierarchies or promised mental superiority are not established facts. Its Roman-life framing and pedagogy need contextual review. Use numbered sections and grammar-topic headings, since web/PDF layout differs. Authored models retain D'Ooge's lexical quantities, including *īnsula* and *nārrō*; keyboard-macron accommodations do not change the model spelling.

First month: pronunciation, nominative/accusative, first-declension singular/plural, present active *amō*, basic agreement. Future topics and readings are mapped in document 03. Correct parsing matters more than guessing an English equivalent. A Latin-capable adult must verify later generated/adapted readings before use.

Modern-language substitution: choose one language, a qualified teacher and a published first-year sequence; protect the same five hours/week all year, with listening, speaking, reading and writing each week. Set novice-level communication goals, quarterly oral samples and an external instructor review. Do not replace a language credit with occasional app use or claim Latin starters teach Spanish. Local acceptance and provider costs remain open.

## Accessibility and edition checks

- Prefer selectable text, heading navigation, reflowable EPUB/HTML, adjustable print, a clear font and sufficient contrast. Check actual files; public-domain status is not accessibility certification.
- Provide lawful text-to-speech/audio, captions, tactile or raised-line graphs, verbal image descriptions and vocabulary preview. Audio access does not remove analytical expectations.
- Offer keyboard alternatives, large-print data sheets and oral response where appropriate; record the skill actually assessed. For writing composition, a scribe/transcription accommodation differs from generating an argument.
- Verify screen-reader math, Latin macrons, image descriptions, subtitles and browser/device behavior with the learner before buying.
- For labs offer seated stations, grip aids, pre-cut samples, prepared slides, recorded partner measurements and equivalent data interpretation. Preserve authentic observation where feasible; disclose when a simulation replaces physical work.
- School staff review IEP/504 or other local supports, tool ages/consent and vendor data practices; this package is not a disability-service plan.
