# Grade 9 Week 10 mathematics: adult guide and keys

**Keep separate from independent learner work.** Five 60-minute sessions,
300 learner minutes. This draft supplies Week 10 mathematics only: it does not
finish Week 10's other courses or certify a complete Grade 9 year.

## Placement, teaching and evidence

Prerequisites: interpret coordinates, substitute signed numbers, make a value
table, and graph a straight line from points. On Day 1, if coordinates and
substitution are not secure, replace the next practice block with a short
coordinate/table model and a fresh check. Do not add a second mathematics hour.
Learners already secure can compare graph estimates with an exact algebraic
solution, but must still explain the common-point interpretation.

Objective `G9-M-W10-common-solution`: independently represent two linear
relations, verify a candidate in both, interpret its domain, and distinguish
unique, absent and coincident-line solutions. This is a course objective,
**not** a newly validated diagnostic skill or automatic mastery score.

Use the separate Wallace reference during explicit teaching on Days 1–2.
It adapts exactly §4.1 Examples 165–166. Its original attribution/license and
change notice must accompany redistribution. All remaining tasks below are
original Bede material. The complete pinned book is local review input, not
permission to activate it in the live tutor.

**Important correction:** two equations are not sufficient by themselves to
guarantee a unique solution; their geometric relationship matters.

## Day-by-day keys and responses

### Day 1

- Guided: `(1,3)` works only in `y=x+2`; `(2,4)` works in both; `(3,5)`
  works only in the first. A correct solution must be the same ordered pair
  in both equations.
- Exit: `(3,5)` satisfies `3+5=8` and `2(3)−5=1`.
- If a learner tests only one coordinate, ask, “What does the ordered pair
  tell you to substitute for each variable?” If only one equation is checked,
  ask, “What would the second relation say at that same point?”

### Day 2

- Guided first-line values at `x=0,1,2,3`: `1,2,3,4`; second-line values:
  `5,4,3,2`. Intersection `(2,3)`: `2+1=3` and `−2+5=3`.
- Exit: `(2,5)`, because `2(2)+1=5` and `−2+7=5`.
- A line is not established by a single plotted point. Check scale and
  labels before treating a wrong graph as an algebra misconception.
- Accessible tables preserve common-pair reasoning but do not certify the
  learner independently drew a graph. Record that distinction.

### Day 3

- Printing model at `x=0,4,8,12,16`: A costs `6,14,22,30,38`; B costs
  `18,22,26,30,34`. They match at `(12,30)`.
- At 8 packets, A costs 22 versus B's 26. At 16 packets, B costs 34 versus
  A's 38. A has the lower initial charge but increases faster.
- Exit: `4+3x=16+x`, so `x=6` and the common cost is 22. For nonnegative
  whole packet counts below 6, C is cheaper; above 6, D is cheaper.
- Counter misconception: the intersection's horizontal coordinate is packet
  count, not dollars. Require units in the explanation.

### Day 4

- `y=2x+1` and `y=2x−3` are distinct parallel lines with vertical separation
  4: no shared point.
- `2y=4x+2` simplifies to `y=2x+1`: same line, infinitely many real
  ordered-pair solutions.
- Classification: unique at `(1,3)`; no solution; infinitely many solutions,
  respectively.
- Accept, for example, `y=x` and `y=x+2` as the new no-solution example.
  Replacing their common slope with another shared value preserves the
  nonzero intercept difference and does not produce an intersection.

## Independent Day 5 key

1. Yes: `2(2)+1=5` and `2+5=7`.
2. `(2,4)`; `−2+6=4` and `2+2=4`. Inspect graph/table and units/labels.
3. No solution: equal slopes, distinct intercepts.
4. Infinitely many solutions: dividing the first equation by 2 yields
   precisely the second relation.
5. `V₁=3+5t`, `V₂=15+2t`; nonnegative whole minutes in the stated
   observation model. At `t=4`, both equal 23 L. At `t=0,1,2,3,4`, the
   first volumes are `3,8,13,18,23`; second volumes `15,17,19,21,23`.
   The task does not establish actual container capacity or physical flow.

Score 2 points per item: 1 for a correct conclusion/representation and 1 for
the required verification or explanation. Record item-level evidence, not
just the 10-point total. Progress provisionally if the learner earns at least
8/10 **and** correctly explains Items 2–4 and formulates both models in Item 5.
These are draft instructional decision rules, not validated mastery thresholds.
If not secure, reteach the failed construct and use a fresh parallel task.
Record supported success separately; do not reclassify corrected work as the
initial independent answer.

## Delayed key and next step

- Intersection `(3,6)`; `3+3=6` and `−3+9=6`.
- Equal slopes 5 and distinct intercepts 2 and −1: no shared point.
- If delayed reasoning is insecure, revisit the relevant representation
  before advancing to the Week 11 substitution method. Do not award durable
  mastery from immediate performance alone.

## Human-led use in both settings

**Homeschool/co-op:** the parent or designated educator teaches the short
models, checks practice and holds the oral conference; a co-op can use shared
seminar time and individually retain independent work. Adult planning estimate:
15 minutes initial preparation, about 15 minutes/day of direct interaction and
checking, and 5 minutes for delayed scoring—approximately 95 minutes total.
This is an unmeasured scheduling estimate, not a tested staffing entitlement.

**Charter classroom/pod:** use a brief teacher model, paired practice, then
individual evidence. The same tasks can be split into 50-minute periods with
the remaining 50 weekly minutes assigned within the school's existing
supervised/homework allocation. The educator approves accommodations, grouping,
local standards mapping and progression. Do not label this state-approved,
credit-bearing or accredited solely because the file exists.

Use only fictional printing/volume data. No names, photographs, accounts,
cloud uploads, physical fluid experiment, or financial transaction is needed.
Graphing tools are optional; paper/table alternatives remain available.
