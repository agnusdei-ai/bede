# Program, placement and delivery

## Purpose and teaching model

Grade 9 joins close reading, ancient world history, mathematical reasoning, empirical biology, sustained language study and introductory computing. Students learn to make a claim, test it, revise it and explain it without outsourcing judgment. Prep-school rigor at a lower total cost is a **design goal**, not a verified comparison, outcome or savings promise.

Adults select texts, teach difficult material, assess work and authorize any tools. The rhythm is explicit instruction → supported practice → independent attempt → oral explanation → feedback → revision → application. A seminar is a discussion with evidence, not an ungraded exchange of opinions. A lab is an investigation with observations, not a video watched alone.

The common academic core is nondevotional. Ancient religious texts are studied as literature and historical evidence, with contextual respect and no required profession of belief. Optional family faith discussions occur separately, do not replace core assignments, and are not required for charter participation.

## Entry readiness and placement

Grade designation is not an age diagnosis. A learner typically enters after Grade 8-level work, but placement follows evidence, accommodations and adult judgment.

- Read a 700–1,000-word passage with annotation and summarize it accurately, using an accessible format if needed.
- Write a coherent paragraph supported by a quotation and attempt a multi-paragraph argument.
- Compute with fractions, signed numbers, ratios and percentages; solve simple equations and interpret a coordinate graph.
- Follow multistep safety instructions, record units, and distinguish observation from inference.
- No previous Latin or coding is required.

**Process:** collect prior work and accommodation information; administer the short diagnostics in document 05 without AI; discuss one problem orally; try two days of appropriately supported work; meet with learner/adult; choose starting level and supports; revisit after weeks 2 and 4. Do not use one score to exclude a learner.

**Algebra default:** adequate arithmetic and emerging equation skills. If foundational gaps persist, begin a 4–6-week bridge with the same math time, replacing rather than stacking new content. Re-map the year with the mathematics teacher; completion date and credit may change.

**Geometry alternative:** only after demonstrated Algebra I proficiency, including factoring, linear systems, functions and quadratics, plus an oral explanation. Prior course completion alone is insufficient. Use the alternative quarter map in document 03 and a selected geometry text/instructor. The Algebra starter is not a Geometry unit.

**Advanced placement:** use a precheck to compact mastered exercises; replace them with deeper proof, original-source comparison or a more complex investigation within the same time budget. Do not add a second workload as a reward for readiness. Provide a supported rereading or shorter equivalent passage when access barriers, rather than reasoning, are the issue.

## End-of-year competencies

By the end of the planned course, the learner should be able to demonstrate:

- **Literature:** interpret a scene in context; distinguish narrator, character and author; sustain a 1,000–1,400-word argument with citations, counterargument and meaningful revision; participate in a text-based seminar.
- **History:** build an accurate comparative chronology; locate ancient societies; explain change using sources and material evidence; compare Mediterranean, African, South Asian, East Asian and American developments without treating one as a universal model.
- **Mathematics:** solve and justify linear equations, inequalities and systems; represent functions; manipulate polynomials, factor and solve introductory quadratics; interpret roots and models with units and limits.
- **Biology:** connect cells, energy, inheritance, evolution and ecology; explain evidence for biological claims; collect measurements safely; analyze variation; write a reproducible report with uncertainty.
- **Latin:** read short adapted passages using first/second/third declension and introductory verb systems; parse forms; explain how morphology changes meaning. This is beginning proficiency, not fluency.
- **Studio:** implement and test a small program; distinguish a rule from a statistical prediction or generated answer; describe data quality, privacy, error and verification; defend a nature-classification project.
- **Habits:** plan work, revise after feedback, credit sources, disclose permitted AI assistance, and explain unaided understanding.

Rubrics and demonstrations supply evidence toward these aims; they are not guaranteed outcomes.

## Courses, credits and time assumptions

Planning convention: **one credit = 180 engaged hours**, half credit = 90; 36 instructional weeks. These are planning units, not universal legal credit definitions. A school or family must reconcile them with local policy, learning evidence and graduation plans.

| Course | Hours/week | Year hours | Planned credit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Literature & Composition I | 5 | 180 | 1 |
| World History I: Ancient Worlds | 5 | 180 | 1 |
| Algebra I OR placed Geometry | 5 | 180 | 1 |
| Biology with Laboratory | 5 | 180 | 1 |
| Latin I OR approved sustained language | 5 | 180 | 1 |
| Logic, Computing & AI Foundations I | 2.5 | 90 | 0.5 |
| PE/health | 2.5 | 90 | 0.5 |
| Practical arts/elective | 2.5 | 90 | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **32.5** | **1,170** | **6.5** |

Five academic credits require 25 hours/week. Three half-credit strands require 7.5. All reading, homework, projects and assessment are included; do not add uncounted nightly work. Meals, breaks, travel and optional clubs are outside 32.5 hours. A five-day week is 6.5 engaged hours/day; with breaks, expect roughly a 7.5–8-hour day. Adjust with the learner rather than treating clock time as proof of mastery.

**Within the 25 core hours:**

- Literature: reading 2h, writing/revision 1.5h, instruction 1h, seminar 0.5h.
- History: reading 2h, source/map work 1h, instruction/discussion 1h, synthesis/assessment 1h.
- Math: instruction 1.5h, guided practice 0.5h, independent practice 2.5h, oral/retrieval check 0.5h.
- Biology: selected reading/concept work 2h, instruction 1h, supervised investigation/data studio 1.5h, notebook/report 0.5h.
- Latin: instruction 1h, structured practice 2h, adapted reading 1h, retrieval/oral work 1h.
- Studio: mini-lesson 0.5h, coding/data practice 1h, project/testing/reflection 1h.
- PE/health: movement 2h plus health learning/planning 0.5h; individualized accessible movement.
- Practical arts: demonstration/planning 0.5h, supervised making/service 1.5h, record/reflection 0.5h.

The 1.5h biology slot occurs every week. Eighteen designated investigations include physical measurement, field observation and explicit models; the other slots are safety practice, data analysis, revision and demonstrations. **54 scheduled lab-studio hours are not 54 hands-on experimental hours.** See the lab ledger before making any laboratory-credit claim.

## Five-day family timetable

Daily core blocks: Literature 60m, History 60m, Math 60m, Biology 60m, Latin 60m = 300m. Add three 30m strands (studio, PE/health, practical arts) = 390m/day. Reading can happen away from screens. A sample sequence is Math → Latin → Literature → lunch → History → Biology → short strands, with breaks between blocks.

- Monday: direct teaching, new reading, math concept, Latin forms, biology concept; plan studio and practical project.
- Tuesday: guided writing/source analysis, math practice, Latin reading, biology preparation; supervised making/movement.
- Wednesday: seminar and argument, math oral check, language retrieval; **90m biology investigation**. Biology uses 30m from Tuesday, whose biology block is 30m; Wednesday is 90m. Weekly biology remains 300m.
- Thursday: drafting, comparison, math application, lab analysis; studio test and health lesson.
- Friday: revision, checks, teach-back, portfolio and weekly planning. No extra “catch-up weekend” assumed.

First-four-week daily agendas in document 04 allocate each course's 300 minutes. Studio has five 30m sessions. PE/arts supply the remaining daily strands. If a co-op lab is held another day, move blocks, not workload.

## Co-op meeting/home-work version

Two meeting days, **Tuesday and Thursday**, each learner has:

- 90m each meeting of literature/history seminar (45m each subject).
- 60m each meeting math.
- 60m each meeting Latin.
- 90m biology Tuesday; 30m biology Thursday.
- 45m studio each meeting.
- 45m practical arts each meeting.
- 30m PE/health each meeting.

Each meeting totals 420m Tuesday and 360m Thursday: **13 hours/week**. Remaining home work is **19.5h**, for the same 32.5h total:
Literature 3.5h, History 3.5h, Math 3h, Latin 3h, Biology 3h, Studio 1h, Arts 1h, PE/health 1.5h.
Schedule home work over Monday/Wednesday/Friday (6.5h each), not as an invisible supplement to full school days. Meeting days still need breaks and travel.

Share a qualified math/language teacher, microscope kits and lab supervisor. Families retain reading support and work checks. A co-op facilitator alone should not claim expertise in every subject.

## School adaptation

Six 50m core/academic periods per day = 25h/week. Allocate five periods/week to each of the five full-credit courses (250m each), plus the five remaining periods rotating studio, PE/health and practical arts (each averages 83⅓m/week on a three-week rotation). Schedule an additional **50m per full-credit course/week** of supervised independent reading/practice (250m total), and **66⅔m per half-credit strand/week** (200m total). These add 450m, or 7.5h, to the 25h school periods: **32.5h total**. Each core reaches 300m and each strand reaches150m. Publish the rotation and homework expectation; this independent work is already included in course budgets, not an extra requirement.

Alternatively, use the family 60m block model within a longer day. Provide one double biology block weekly by exchanging a period, not omitting another subject. Schools approve standards, prerequisites, credit titles and graduation fit; do not assume a 6.5-credit plan meets a particular state's requirements.

## Human workload and support

Planning estimates, not promises: family lead 8–12h/week of direct teaching, feedback and checking, plus 2–4h preparation; the supervised lab may overlap instruction but needs uninterrupted attention. A learner needing substantial reading, executive-function or language support may require more adult time. In a new co-op, budget each specialist roughly 1–2 preparation/feedback hours per contact hour until real workload is measured. Do not double-count simultaneous teaching as independent adult availability.

Parents can lead reading, discussion, organization, retrieval practice and low-risk measurement after preparation. Secure qualified assistance for persistent math difficulties, Latin accuracy, unfamiliar biological concepts, safety supervision and accessibility needs. Set group size from the supervisor's actual ability to observe every learner and equipment station, not an invented universal ratio.

## Inclusions, exclusions and graduation planning

Included: this curriculum map, nine-week teaching materials, placement and assessment templates, basic investigations and no-paid-AI programming route. Not included: accredited enrollment, transcripts, automatic grades, individualized special education services, complete weeks 10–36 lessons, purchased books, devices, full laboratory facilities, AI subscriptions or a qualified teacher.

Logic is practiced through claims/evidence/counterarguments in literature/history; formal truth conditions, algorithms and tests belong to the half-credit studio. One artifact can be discussed in two classes, but record its time and primary grade once. No second “Logic I” credit is claimed.

Plan later years for Geometry/Algebra II and beyond, further laboratory sciences, modern world/US history and civics as locally required, additional language, arts, health/PE, career exploration and electives. This Grade 9 plan does not complete high-school graduation requirements.
