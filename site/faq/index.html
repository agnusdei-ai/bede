Books, technology, and the place of the screen

Classical studies & modern tools

More thinking. Not more screen time.

Literature and mathematics belong together. So do history, science, art, and nature. Technology should help a child engage with those subjects—not replace the book, the notebook, the teacher, or the world outside. The family experience below shows how that works at home.

Keep the human work

  • Read closely. Attend to the actual text, not just a summary.
  • Write clearly. Put an idea into your own words, then revise it.
  • Reason carefully. Explain why an answer makes sense and consider alternatives.
  • Apply thoughtfully. Use a concept in a new problem, discussion, or everyday decision with adult guidance.

Give technology a supporting role

  • Asks, then sends outside. A Socratic conversation, then narration, handwriting, and nature study away from the screen. It introduces the lesson. It does not consume it.
  • Follows your direction. Parents choose the subjects and books, review the work, and decide the next step.
  • Keeps the outdoors and the real book at the center. Nature study and living books are subjects in their own right, not a break from the "real" lesson.
  • Shows work for review. Learning records help an adult decide what needs another explanation or a different application.

Household sessions connect to your home-network host. Away-from-home use requires separately configured, approved access. Sessions need a connection; voice and handwriting vary by device, browser, and permissions.

Plainly Stated

What Bede does, and what it will not do

The studio is the outdoors and the book, never the screen. Bede introduces a concept and helps reinforce it, then sends your child back to the real work: telling it back aloud, writing and drawing it by hand, and remembering it later.

What it does

  • Teaches from your books. You choose the subjects and texts. Bede anchors the lesson on what you are already reading.
  • Asks, rather than tells. Socratic questions paced for the Grammar and Logic stages, backing off to a hint after a round or two rather than pressing.
  • Works in hand and voice. Narration aloud, writing and drawing by hand, and a check later on what actually stuck.
  • Keeps a real record. Narration history; mastery in mathematics, reading foundations, reading & spelling, writing, language exposure and music knowledge; and a summary after every sitting.
  • Runs on your hardware. Windows, macOS, or Linux, with an installer for each, on a computer you already own. Encrypted at rest, in your house, optionally with the model itself local so no lesson text leaves at all.

What it will not do

  • Act as a spiritual advisor. Doctrine and pastoral counsel stay with you and your own pastor, priest, or minister.
  • Score your child's faith. No metric on prayer, no progress bar on belief: a written rule, not a setting.
  • Replace you. The parent is the curriculum director. Bede implements your plan. It does not overrule your judgment.
  • Claim what it has not earned. No invented certainty, no invented evidence, and no pretending to remember a conversation it never had.
  • Hold on to your data. One button deletes a student completely: transcripts, narrations, mastery, voice profile. Not archived.

In Practice

Speaking and writing, the way a child actually can

A seven-year-old can say a full sentence long before they can type one. Bede is built around that fact rather than around a keyboard.

Talk instead of type

Press and hold to speak an answer, the same gesture as a walkie-talkie, or switch on "Voice on" mode to go fully hands-free. A child who can say the answer does not need to spell it first.

A real page to write and draw on

When Bede asks for a narration, math worked out, or a nature sketch, a full page opens on the tablet: pencil, pen, and eraser, ruled to match the work — composition lines, graph paper, music staff, or blank — sized like a real sheet of paper.

Bede knows who's speaking

Each child has a short voice check at the start of a session, so a young reader is never stuck typing a PIN. It's a convenience, not a lock: you can always step in and override it.

The method

Why classical instead of a skill-tree, STEM-drilling app?

Because those two things are answering different questions. A skill-tree app is optimized to get a child through a math or science checkpoint as efficiently as possible, and it is honestly good at that. It is not built to ask whether the child is becoming patient, honest, or attentive along the way. A dashboard can grade a multiple-choice answer. It cannot grade a conscience, so that part is left for someone else to worry about.

Bede is built to hold both at once. Academics still get real, recorded progress. Mathematics, composition, reading, and more all show up on the Progress page. But faith, character, and discipline sit beside that on purpose, governed by Bede's own written constitution rather than left as a marketing claim. Comprehension, Compassion, and Conscience run through every subject, not only the academic ones. And the screen is deliberately not where the day lives. Bede introduces a lesson and helps reinforce it, then sends the child back to the real book, the nature notebook, and the outdoors, the same as the classical tradition always has.

What does "classical" actually mean here?

Three stages, matched to how children actually change rather than to a school calendar. In the Grammar stage a child's memory is enormous and their appetite is for facts, names, stories, songs, and dates, so that is what you feed it. In the Logic stage, somewhere around eleven, the same child starts arguing with everything. That is not defiance. It is the arrival of the faculty you now teach directly: how claims connect, what follows from what, where an argument breaks. The Rhetoric stage that follows is about saying it well and saying it truly.

Bede paces itself to these stages, not just to a grade number. Younger children get one simple, single-idea question at a time. Older ones get counterexamples and are asked to defend a position. Alongside that sit the two habits the tradition rests on: living books rather than textbooks (real authors writing about things they loved), and narration, where the child tells the passage back in their own words instead of answering a worksheet about it.

Why the Socratic method: does asking really beat telling?

Because remembering is not a readout, it is a rebuild. Every time a child reconstructs an idea instead of receiving it, the path back to it gets stronger. That is why narration (tell me that chapter back in your own words) does more than re-reading the chapter, and why an answer a child generates is held far better than the same answer handed over. Re-reading feels productive and mostly is not. The effort of retrieval is not a cost of the method. It is the method working.

The "why" and "how" do a second job. A child can hold a great many whats without any of them connecting to each other, and disconnected facts do not transfer to new problems. Questions that push on reasoning force the connections that make knowledge usable somewhere other than where it was learned.

In practice Bede never simply gives the answer. It asks the question your child's actual words invite, follows one thread rather than five, and after a round or two of follow-ups offers a hint or moves on rather than pressing. A stuck child needs a foothold, not another question. Its own instruction puts it plainly: a child who discovers a truth owns it. A child who is told a truth only borrows it.

To be careful about what we are claiming: these are long-standing findings about how people learn, and a tradition with a very long track record. They are not a study of Bede. We have said elsewhere on this page that we do not have one yet.

Does my child type, or can they just talk and write it out?

Mostly neither, on purpose. A child presses and holds a button to speak an answer, or switches on "Voice on" mode so Bede listens for them to finish and responds without another tap. Typing a full sentence is a separate skill from having the thought, and Bede is built to test for the second one.

For narration, math work, or a nature sketch, the tablet opens a full page to write or draw on by hand: pencil, pen, and eraser, ruled to match the work, sized like a real sheet. It is deliberately not a text box.

At the start of a session Bede also checks each child's voice against a short phrase they recorded once, so a six-year-old is not stuck typing a PIN to get started. That's for convenience, not security — a parent can always step in and override it.

Why so much emphasis on discipline and attention?

Because everything above depends on it, and none of it survives a divided mind. Retrieval is effortful by design. A child who is half-present cannot do it, and a lesson delivered to a wandering attention has not happened, however long it lasted. This is why lessons here are short (fifteen to twenty-five minutes a subject) rather than long. Full attention for twenty minutes beats partial attention for an hour, and it is kinder besides.

The older tradition treats attention as a habit to be formed rather than a trait a child either has or lacks, and formed the way any habit is: in small, repeated, unexciting doses, protected by the adults around the child. It is not hostility to entertainment. It is that consolidation happens after the lesson, in the quiet, and a mind returned to a fast screen every evening never gets that hour.

The pod

What is a pod, and do we need more than one child?

A pod is whoever learns together under one plan, up to ten. That might be your own household, a small class, a co-op group that meets twice a week, or a tutor's cohort. Bede does not care which, and does not ask. It has no notion of who is related to whom.

If you have one child, you have a pod of one, and nothing in the method needs a second. Everything below is about what changes when there is more than one, not about what you are missing if there is not.

How does onboarding actually work?

As a real orientation, not a five-minute sales call, regardless of size. We sit down with you, walk through setup together, and train everyone on how Bede actually works in one session — whether that is a single homeschool family, a large household with several children, or a whole co-op.

For a co-op, that session covers every family in the group at once, rather than repeating the same walkthrough family by family. Write to info@agnusdei.ai, tell us which of the three describes you and roughly how many people, and we will schedule a time that works.

Why does a questioning method suit children of different ages in one room?

Because of what questioning is. A lesson that is delivered has to be pitched at a level: one explanation, one vocabulary, one pace. In a mixed-age room that is always wrong for somebody. A lesson that is asked cannot be pitched that way at all. The next question depends on the answer the child in front of you just gave, so it lands where they actually are rather than where the average of the room is.

That is why each child in a pod has their own subject list, their own grade, their own pacing and their own session length. Two children can be at the same table on the same morning and not be in the same lesson. The six-year-old is asked one concrete thing at a time and answers out loud. The eleven-year-old is handed a counterexample and asked to defend a position. Neither is waiting for the other.

The classical tradition has a name for this staging: Grammar, Logic, Rhetoric. Bede paces itself to the stage rather than to a grade number. Mixed ages are the normal case here, not an accommodation.

My child got stuck and looked confused. Is the method failing?

Usually it is the method working, and this is the single most useful thing to understand before you watch a session. The old word for that moment is aporia, the point where a child's own explanation stops holding together and they can see that it does. It is uncomfortable, and it is where the thinking starts. A child who is never confused is being told things.

What matters is that nobody is left there. Bede does not keep pressing: after a round or two of follow-ups on the same idea it simplifies, offers a hint, or moves on, because a stuck child needs a foothold rather than another question. For the youngest it is gentler still: one single-idea question at a time, and usually one follow-up before backing off. This is guided questioning, not abandonment dressed up as discovery.

Your part is the hard part: wait a little longer than is comfortable before you rescue. The rescue is the thing that ends the learning, and it is very hard not to give. What you are training, across a year of these moments, is a child who meets something they do not understand and stays with it, rather than one who reads confusion as proof they are not clever. That is what "teaching a child how to learn" actually consists of.

Does Bede compare my children to each other?

No, and it is built so that it cannot. Where the pod's record could have become a table of who is ahead, there is deliberately no score, no ordering by any measure, and no per-child total anywhere, not even one worked out on the page. Children are listed alphabetically so the order cannot shift when the amounts do, and a child who has not yet worked on something is simply absent from it rather than shown beside a sibling at zero.

The distinction we hold to is between two questions that look similar and are not. Who has done this work is an ordinary thing for a team to know, and it is how one child comes to help another. Who is better at this is not built, in any screen, for anyone.

Children never see the pod record at all. It is yours. A child who can see they are behind a sibling has been ranked, whatever the screen is called.

In a pod, what is my job and what is Bede's?

You are the curriculum director, for every child in it. You choose the subjects, the books, the topics for the term, the pacing and the limits, per child. Bede works that plan and does not overrule your judgment. That is written into its constitution, not left to a setting.

Bede's job is narrower than it first appears: to ask the question your child's actual answer invites, and then to stay out of the way. Its own constitution puts the discipline plainly: "do the difficult good patiently without taking the learner's work away from them." Taking the work away is the failure mode it is written against, and it is the same temptation you will feel at the table.

The child's job is the thinking, and it cannot be delegated to either of you. That is the whole arrangement.

Faith

Do we have to be Catholic to use Bede?

No. Scripture & Bible Study and Saints & Catechism are two separate subjects, and you enable either, both, or neither. Scripture is deliberately denomination-neutral: Bible narrative, memory verses, and the moral law the text plainly teaches, without asserting one tradition's doctrine or canon as though every Christian holds it. Saints & Catechism is the Catholic module and openly so. Tell Bede your family's church tradition and the Bible translation you already read, and it frames lessons to match.

One honest limit on the translation setting. Bede quotes the public-domain translations (the King James and the Douay-Rheims) directly. The other nine are modern, actively copyrighted translations that Bede has no licensed copy of, so for those it paraphrases and gives you the book, chapter, and verse to look up, rather than presenting remembered wording as an exact quotation. It is the difference between a tutor who says "look this up in your own Bible" and one that quietly invents a verse.

Will Bede teach our children doctrine we disagree with?

Bede is not a spiritual advisor and must never become one. Doctrine, moral counsel, and pastoral judgment stay with you and with your own pastor, priest, deacon, or minister. When a child asks something that belongs to them, Bede says so and sends it home.

Bede also never scores, measures, or quantifies your child's faith. There is no engagement metric on prayer and no progress bar on belief. That is written into Bede's constitution as a rule, not left to a setting.

Curriculum

Which curriculum comes with Bede, and can we keep our own?

Bede comes with a living-books catalog covering Years 1 through 8 in the Charlotte Mason tradition, inspired by Mater Amabilis: Aesop, Kipling, and Milne in the early years, through Plutarch, Shakespeare, Austen, Dickens, and Chesterton by eighth grade. The Catholic catechism module draws on Ignatius Press's Faith and Life series.

Yes, and that is the intended way to use Bede. Tell it what you are reading or working through, and it teaches into that lesson rather than around it. Families working from Memoria Press, Classical Academic Press, Well-Trained Mind Press, the Institute for Excellence in Writing, RightStart Mathematics, or Logic of English can point Bede at today's page and keep their own spine.

Set the whole thing to Book Companion and Bede does nothing but discuss the book in your hands, with a light touch and few interruptions. Add structure later, only if you want it.

Do you teach Latin, Greek, and Logic?

Yes, all three, as their own subjects. They are electives rather than part of the default day: nothing turns them on but you, and the core Charlotte Mason rotation does not quietly grow because we shipped something new. The languages run ten minutes a sitting, Logic fifteen.

Latin & Christian Foundations is built on the vocabulary every Christian tradition already shares: fides, spes, caritas, sapientia, veritas, hanging on Christ's own summary of the whole law in the Latin of the Vulgate. It sits beside Scripture rather than inside the Catholic module on purpose: a family holding none of the distinctively Catholic doctrines can teach it start to finish and never meet them. Every Latin word and verse was checked against published Vulgate editions when it was written, and Bede quotes from that fixed text rather than improvising. In an inflected language one wrong ending is a wrong meaning, and neither child nor parent should have to catch it. Pronunciation is Ecclesiastical and said to be. If your own program teaches Classical, Bede does not correct your child.

Greek & New Testament Foundations is the companion, and answers what Latin structurally cannot: the Vulgate is a translation, and Koine Greek is the language the New Testament was actually written in. It is also the classical language that serves Orthodox families, who are served by neither the Catholic module nor the Western Church's Latin. Greek always appears with its transliteration and its English beside it. Four of its six terms carry the same anchor verse as their Latin counterpart, so a child taking both meets one verse in two languages and can see for themselves that caritas is translating ἀγάπη (agapē). For the youngest, the alphabet is the curriculum: a couple of letters a week, not all twenty-four at once.

Logic starts in third grade and never earlier. Formal reasoning handed to a six-year-old is exactly the premature abstraction the classical tradition warns about, so for K–2 the subject is not offered at all. Grades three to five get the moves without the vocabulary. Six to eight get named fallacies and worked syllogisms, each with its verdict stated, including the case that matters most: an argument can be perfectly valid and still carry you to a false conclusion, because one of the things it started from was not true. Three limits travel with it. Logic serves truth, never winning. Bede will not coach a child in arguing against their own parents, and redirects to stating the other side's strongest case first. And it rules on no contested political, moral, or religious dispute however logically it is dressed up. The form is taught with deliberately dull examples, and the substance goes to you and your own pastor, priest, or minister.

Your part in it

Does Bede replace me as the teacher?

No. You set the subjects, the texts, the daily plan, and the boundaries, and you can preview any response in a private sandbox before your child ever sees it. Bede treats the parent as the curriculum director and does not override that judgment.

What does this ask of me as the parent?

More than a subscription, and we would rather be honest about that than sell you something that quietly fails. Plan on roughly two focused hours a day, and the discipline to protect them: a steady place to work, the books to hand, and the day arranged so the work actually happens.

Those two hours are a floor for focused work, not a ceiling on learning. Nothing here caps how much a child reads, narrates, builds, or explores. Time with books and time with Bede can both run well past it, and often should. What is bounded is continuous screen time, not learning.

Encourage generous reading and time away from the screen, so children can reflect on and apply what they learn.

You choose the subjects, the texts, and the learning outcomes. Bede supports the practice; you guide the learning and review progress together.

Progress and evidence

What does Bede actually measure?

Mastery in mathematics, composition, reading and spelling, and language exposure, all of it gathered from the ordinary work of the lesson rather than from quizzes. A narration given, a problem reasoned through, a word sounded out. The session summary reports real movement, and the Progress page shows the record behind it.

Reading is measured differently either side of third grade, because it is a different skill either side of third grade. In K–2 it is decoding: sounds, blends, the patterns that turn letters into a word. From third grade on it is comprehension, vocabulary, inference, and spelling, which English makes its own subject rather than an afterthought. A child sees the picture that fits where they actually are, never both at once.

Has this been tested with other families?

Not yet, and we will not pretend otherwise. Bede has been built and tested in the founder's own family, which is a sample of one. The public beta exists precisely to find out whether what works at our kitchen table works at yours.

So the page above describes what Bede does and what it refuses to do (both checkable the day you install it) rather than results we have not earned the right to claim. When we have real outcomes across real families, we will publish them, including the parts that did not work.

Safety, privacy, and screens

How does Bede support independent thinking?

Bede is instructed to guide active learning tasks with questions and conceptual hints, leaving the learner to work out the answer. A parent or educator can ask for a teaching goal, three progressive questions, and adaptations for support, on-level practice, or extension.

Ask the learner to explain the evidence and apply the idea in a new situation. The adult reviews the reasoning and decides what comes next.

Can a school or family use an offline installation?

An offline installation requires locally provisioned models and an isolated network setup. Its offline mode restricts the API to local services and denies cloud model fallback. The hosted public demo uses a separate provider configuration.

Reviewed curriculum search is being developed separately from live tutoring. It favors primary sources and reviewed learning objects matched to the learning goal and grade; TEKS or Common Core alignment requires an explicit reviewed mapping. We do not infer alignment from a document's topic.

Request an implementation conversation to review local hardware, model availability, privacy controls, and the school's requirements before using student information.

Where does our data live, and can I delete it?

In your own database, on a machine in your own house. Voice profiles, student plans, transcripts, and assessments are AES-256-GCM encrypted at rest, so the database itself never holds plaintext. You also get narration history, mastery snapshots, session summaries, and an encrypted audit log: a real record for a real school year.

That is your choice, and it is a real one. Point Bede at a language model running on your own machine and no lesson text leaves at all. Choose a cloud provider instead and the lesson text goes to that provider. You can see which one is active and switch it from the parent screen, mid-day, without touching a config file or restarting anything.

How is the parent account itself protected?

The same way we would want ours protected. A password is required to sign in as the parent, and you can add a security key or an authenticator app as a second factor whenever you want one. If you ever lose access, recovery requires two of three separate proofs, not one, so a single leaked secret can't let someone else in on its own.

Sensitive actions — viewing the audit log, changing the AI provider, deleting a student's data outright — ask for your password again in the moment, even mid-session. Being logged in for the morning is not the same thing as being able to do anything on the account, and Bede treats the two differently by default.

If a tablet is lost, stolen, or just retired, you can revoke that one device from the parent screen. It stops working immediately. Every other device in the house keeps going.

What happens if my child says something worrying?

Every message is checked before it reaches the tutor. If a child expresses distress, fear, or danger, Bede stops the lesson rather than teaching through it, and tells them to find a parent or trusted adult right now. Clear attempts to talk Bede out of its own rules are redirected on the spot. Subtler probing is logged, and a sustained pattern emails you.

How much screen time is this?

Less than you might expect, and bounded by design. Sessions stop on their own (two hours by default, four at the absolute maximum), and a ten-minute break runs after every hour regardless. You can set a tighter cap with a longer eye-rest break on top of that, and for the youngest learners the day is paced in twenty-minute blocks.

Learning continues beyond the device. Children can read, narrate, build, and explore throughout the day. A short session with Bede supports that work rather than replacing it.

A little device time. A great deal of reading.

Getting started

What do we need to run it?

A computer you already own. There is an installer for Windows, one for macOS on both Apple Silicon and Intel, and one for Linux. Bede runs on that machine in your house rather than on someone else's server. The children then connect to it from their own tablets or from whatever device is free.

Two ways to provide the intelligence, and you can change your mind later from the parent screen without touching a config file. Point Bede at a cloud provider and lessons go to that provider. Run a model on the same machine instead and no lesson text leaves the house at all. The local option asks more of the hardware, which is the honest trade. The installers will offer to set it up and choose a model sized to the computer you actually have.
