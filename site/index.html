What Bede does, and what it will not do

The studio is the outdoors and the book — never the screen. Bede introduces a concept and helps reinforce it, then sends your child back to the real work: telling it back aloud, writing and drawing it by hand, and remembering it later.

What it does

  • Teaches from your books. You choose the subjects and texts; Bede anchors the lesson on what you are already reading.
  • Asks, rather than tells. Socratic questions paced for the Grammar and Logic stages, backing off to a hint after a round or two rather than pressing.
  • Works in hand and voice. Narration aloud, writing and drawing by hand, and a check later on what actually stuck.
  • Keeps a real record. Narration history, mastery in mathematics, composition, K–2 reading, and language exposure, and a summary after every sitting.
  • Runs on your hardware. Encrypted at rest, on a machine in your house — optionally with the model itself local, so no lesson text leaves at all.

What it will not do

  • Act as a spiritual advisor. Doctrine and pastoral counsel stay with you and your own pastor, priest, or minister.
  • Score your child's faith. No metric on prayer, no progress bar on belief — a written rule, not a setting.
  • Replace you. The parent is the curriculum director. Bede implements your plan; it does not overrule your judgment.
  • Claim what it has not earned. No invented certainty, no invented evidence, and no pretending to remember a conversation it never had.
  • Hold on to your data. One button deletes a student completely — transcripts, narrations, mastery, voice profile. Not archived.

What We Are Aiming For

Expected outcomes, and where they come from

These are the outcomes Bede is built to produce, tested in one family — the founder's own. Not a study, not a cohort, not yet a claim about your children. That is what the beta is for, and we would rather say so than dress up a sample of one.

Knowledge that stays

Retention is the point, so it is worked at directly: narration in the child's own words, writing and drawing by hand, and check-backs on what was taught earlier rather than moving on and never asking again. What held is recorded, not assumed.

Digital instruction, kept in its place

The screen introduces a concept and helps reinforce it; the reading, narrating, and observing happen away from it. The guardrails are not a policy page — Bede's limits live in a written constitution that is verified every time the software starts.

A program you co-lead

You name up to three mastery outcomes per core area each term. Bede steers sessions toward them and records the evidence, and the Progress page groups the concepts your child actually demonstrated subject by subject.

Mastery is tracked for each child who engages with the work. Bede can invite a reluctant learner, and it is patient about it, but it cannot compel one — and a tutor that claimed otherwise would be lying to you.

Questions Families Ask

Before you begin

Do we have to be Catholic to use Bede?

No. Scripture & Bible Study and Saints & Catechism are two separate subjects, and you enable either, both, or neither. Scripture is deliberately denomination-neutral — Bible narrative, memory verses, and the moral law the text plainly teaches, without asserting one tradition's doctrine or canon as though every Christian holds it. Saints & Catechism is the Catholic module and openly so. Tell Bede your family's church tradition and the Bible translation you already read, and it frames lessons to match.

Will Bede teach our children doctrine we disagree with?

Bede is not a spiritual advisor and must never become one. Doctrine, moral counsel, and pastoral judgment stay with you and with your own pastor, priest, deacon, or minister. When a child asks something that belongs to them, Bede says so and sends it home.

Bede also never scores, measures, or quantifies your child's faith. There is no engagement metric on prayer and no progress bar on belief. That is written into Bede's constitution as a rule, not left to a setting.

Which curriculum comes with Bede?

A living-books catalog covering Years 1 through 8 in the Charlotte Mason tradition, inspired by Mater Amabilis — Aesop, Kipling, and Milne in the early years, through Plutarch, Shakespeare, Austen, Dickens, and Chesterton by eighth grade. The Catholic catechism module draws on Ignatius Press's Faith and Life series.

Can we keep using the curriculum we already own?

Yes, and that is the intended way to use Bede. Tell it what you are reading or working through and it teaches into that lesson rather than around it — families working from Memoria Press, Classical Academic Press, Well-Trained Mind Press, the Institute for Excellence in Writing, RightStart Mathematics, or Logic of English can point Bede at today's page and keep their own spine.

Set the whole thing to Book Companion and Bede does nothing but discuss the book in your hands, with a light touch and few interruptions. Add structure later, only if you want it.

Does Bede replace me as the teacher?

No. You set the subjects, the texts, the daily plan, and the boundaries, and you can preview any response in a private sandbox before your child ever sees it. Bede treats the parent as the curriculum director and does not override that judgment.

What does Bede actually measure?

Mastery in mathematics, composition, K–2 reading foundations, and language exposure — all of it gathered from the ordinary work of the lesson rather than from quizzes. A narration given, a problem reasoned through, a word sounded out. The session summary reports real movement, and the Progress page shows the record behind it.

Where does our data live?

In your own database, on a machine in your own house. Voice profiles, student plans, transcripts, and assessments are AES-256-GCM encrypted at rest, so the database itself never holds plaintext. You also get narration history, mastery snapshots, session summaries, and an encrypted audit log — a real record for a real school year.

Does anything leave the house?

That is your choice, and it is a real one. Point Bede at a language model running on your own machine and no lesson text leaves at all. Choose a cloud provider instead and the lesson text goes to that provider — you can see which one is active and switch it from the parent screen, mid-day, without touching a config file or restarting anything.

What happens if my child says something worrying?

Every message is checked before it reaches the tutor. If a child expresses distress, fear, or danger, Bede stops the lesson rather than teaching through it, and tells them to find a parent or trusted adult right now. Clear attempts to talk Bede out of its own rules are redirected on the spot; subtler probing is logged, and a sustained pattern emails you.

How much screen time is this?

Less than you might expect, and bounded by design. Sessions stop on their own — two hours by default, four at the absolute maximum — and a ten-minute break runs after every hour regardless. You can set a tighter cap with a longer eye-rest break on top of that, and for the youngest learners the day is paced in twenty-minute blocks.

Worth separating from the two hours a day the guarantee asks for: that figure is the whole learning day, not screen time. Most of it is reading, narrating aloud, writing, drawing, and going outside to look at something. The screen introduces a concept and helps reinforce it, and then the child leaves it.

What do I need to run it?

A computer in your house — Windows, Mac, or Linux, including a Raspberry Pi. There are installers for each. Children connect from their own tablets over your home network, and running a lesson never requires a call back to us.

Can I delete everything?

Yes. One type-to-confirm button removes everything belonging to a student — transcripts, narrations, mastery, voice profile, the lot. Not archived. Gone.

Has this been tested with other families?

Not yet, and we will not pretend otherwise. Bede has been built and tested in the founder's own family, which is a sample of one. The public beta exists precisely to find out whether what works at our kitchen table works at yours.

So the page above describes what Bede does and what it refuses to do — both checkable the day you install it — rather than results we have not earned the right to claim. When we have real outcomes across real families, we will publish them, including the parts that did not work.

What is the learner's guarantee?

Choose one topic at the start. Over the next thirty days, you set it as the curriculum giver and Bede works it as the hands-on tutor — focused study, daily practice, coordinated between you. If your learner has not reached mastery of that topic by day thirty, we refund the annual cost in full, coaching included. The coaching is the part families value most and it is not carved out.

What counts as mastery is not our opinion on the day you ask. The topic you chose appears in your own Progress page under Term Mastery Outcomes, and moves from Not started through Introduced and Developing to Mastered on evidence Bede records silently during ordinary lessons — no test, no separate assessment. You are reading the same screen we are.

With focused daily lessons we expect a topic to take about two weeks. Thirty days is deliberate headroom, not the target — a guarantee you have to squeak past is not much of a guarantee.

Two things this asks of you, said plainly rather than discovered later. It needs the daily practice: a topic worked twice in thirty days is not a fair test of anything, and we will say so. And it needs a learner who engages — Bede is patient with a reluctant one, but it cannot compel attention, and no honest guarantee can promise otherwise.

What does this ask of me as the parent?

More than a subscription, and we would rather be honest about that than sell you something that quietly fails. Plan on roughly two hours a day of genuine learning time, and the discipline to protect it: a steady place to work, the books to hand, and the day arranged so the work actually happens.

Those two hours are the whole learning day, not two hours at a screen. Most of it is reading, narrating aloud, writing, drawing, and going outside to look at something. Bede's own share is a portion of it, and a bounded one.

You remain the curriculum giver: you choose the subjects, the texts, and the topic under the guarantee. Bede is the hands-on tutor working that plan daily. It is a real division of labour, and it does not work if either half goes missing.
