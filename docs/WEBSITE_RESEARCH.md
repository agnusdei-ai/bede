Public route: `/research/`. Scope: evidence for the tutoring method, not an
evaluation of Bede. The owner requested brief copy without inline citations,
academic footnotes, or long study summaries. Supporting evidence is retained
here for review, not added to public copy. Reviewed October 20, 2026.

## Claim audit

| Public statement | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|
| A 2006 review of 37 studies found generally positive parent-tutoring effects, mostly in primary-grade reading. | [Erion, 2006, ERIC EJ744802](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ744802). Includes 20 group-design and 17 single-subject studies. Do not call all 37 randomized trials, extrapolate to every K–8 subject, or convert the reported +0.55 standardized group effect into a percent gain. |
| Structured tutoring has positive learning effects. | [Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan, 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00028312231208687), published PreK–12 randomized-field-experiment synthesis, pooled 0.288 SD; [Erion, 2006](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ744802); [Sénéchal & Young, 2008](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654308320319), 16 literacy-intervention studies, 1,340 K–3 families. Do not mix the 2020 tutoring working-paper estimate with the 2024 journal estimate. |
| Instructional guidance supports recall, explanation, and revisiting ideas. | [IES, Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning, 2007](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf): retrieval/quizzing and explanatory questions, strong evidence; spacing, moderate evidence. Primarily grades 3–12. These mechanisms do not validate every feature of classical narration or a complete curriculum. |
| A 2024 Tutor CoPilot trial found 4 percentage points higher lesson-topic mastery when AI supported tutors. | [ERIC ED661562](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED661562), academic working-paper record: randomized live tutoring with 900 tutors and 1,800 K–12 students. The 4-point figure is the overall effect; do not substitute the up-to-9-point subgroup finding or call this a 4% gain, parent-tutoring trial, or independent long-term retention result. |
| Harvard: 194 undergraduate physics students, stronger immediate learning with the research-designed AI tutor than in-class active learning. | [Kestin et al., Scientific Reports, June 3, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6); [Harvard institutional record](https://serl.fas.harvard.edu/publication/ai-tutoring-outperforms-class-active-learning-rct-introducing-novel-research-based). Two lessons in consecutive weeks; randomized crossover at peer-group level. Not a K–8 or parent-led study, and not evidence about all AI tools or long-term learning. |
| First Avenue School documents Khanmigo use with educators in grades 5–8. | [Newark Board of Education official report](https://www.nps.k12.nj.us/press-releases/bill-gates-visits-first-avenue-school-commends-innovative-use-of-technology), search-index date May 10, 2024; publication date not exposed in fetched body. [Later official district report](https://www.nps.k12.nj.us/press-releases/newark-public-schools-welcomes-aft-president-randi-weingarten-and-demonstrates-ai-powered-learning), search-index date May 26, 2026. These document adoption and commentary, not controlled learning effects. |

## Evidence considered that constrains the copy

- [Parental Homework Involvement and Students' Achievement, PubMed 38227295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38227295/): correlational meta-analysis, 28 studies and 378,222 participants; weak negative overall relationship, with autonomy support positively related to achievement. Do not imply that more parental homework involvement automatically helps or that these associations are causal.
- [Bastani et al., PNAS, 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122): one Turkish high-school mathematics experiment; ordinary GPT-4 assistance improved practice performance but unassisted examination performance was 17% lower than control. Teacher-informed guardrails removed the negative effect, not demonstrated improvement above control. Supports caution about answer-giving, not a claim of universally effective guarded AI.
- [Oreopoulos & Low, NBER Working Paper 35620, August 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620): two-year cluster-randomized study in 18 Tennessee middle schools; assignment to Khan Academy plus Khanmigo produced 0.06–0.08 SD over a school year. The abstract says gains resemble Khan Academy practice without AI. Do not attribute the package's gains uniquely to AI.
- [Access Is Not Enough, ERIC ED681902, June 2026](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681902): working-paper record reports tutor support increased engagement with an elementary AI literacy platform but did not improve reading achievement. Engagement and academic learning are distinct outcomes.

## Content and appearance contracts

- No product-performance claim, guaranteed gain, school endorsement, partnership,
  or subjective ranking such as “advanced school.”
- Institution examples are explicitly independent and not affiliations.
- No visible citations, bibliography, external study links, trackers, or new form.
- Keep public main copy below 325 words, with short sections and the shared
  ink/gold CTA treatment. Do not turn it into a literature review.
- Link `/research/` once in all eleven marketing footers and list it in the
  sitemap. Retain even two-column mobile rows and visible keyboard focus.
- Recheck sources before changing numerical claims. Older foundational research
  is dated honestly; no numerical conversion into months or percentage gains.
